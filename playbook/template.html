What the research found

  1. The hook is the conversion, not the number. "How much is a billion dollars?" managed 3.6× on a 1.5M-subscriber channel. The same idea as a unit ("Cost in Units of Starbucks Lattes", 9.9M views) or as time ("$1 a second for a billion = 32 years of your life", 9.4M at 151×) breaks out.
  2. Small accounts break out because of the format, not the audience. On IG and TikTok, accounts under 5K followers had a 174× median outlier, against 19× for 500K+ accounts. On YouTube, channels with 841 to 15K subscribers reached 1.7M to 22.8M views.
  3. Winners are very short or long, rarely in between. They are either 4–8 s read-it loops or 45–155 s walkthroughs where every sentence adds a new number.
  4. Almost nobody shows the math, and much of what is shown is wrong. A visible, sourced, rough-but-right calculation is a position nobody holds.
  5. "Envelope" has demand on both sides. There is Fermi-style "cost in units of…" estimation, and there is #cashstuffing at about 1.9B TikTok views, where nobody does the math.
  6. The platforms now penalise templates and reward originality (YouTube's Oct 1, 2026 Shorts update and Instagram's April 2026 aggregator rule). Our own hand-drawn calculations are both the brand and the protection.

How this was made. We swept vidIQ outlier indexes for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok (about 109 calls and 582 credits), mined 16 finance creators' catalogues, ran more than 100 web searches on platform mechanics and trends, and watched 22 outliers scene by scene. Three independent strategy panels then proposed shortlists, and a judge merged them. Each format got a writer, who produced three teasers, and an independent fact-checker and editor, who re-derived every number and re-verified every source. The animatics come from engine/, a renderer written from scratch for this channel. Everything lives in the repo: research/, teasers/, brand/envelope-math-format-bible.md and engine/specs/. Run cd engine && npm install && npm run render to re-render every teaser at 1080×1920.

Educational math, not financial advice. Real-world figures are sourced and dated in each teaser's write-up as of October 2026.
