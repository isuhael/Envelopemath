# Top 10 approaches for Back of the Envelope (Envelope Math)

**Date:** 2026-10-07 · **Companion report:** [`01-viral-finance-shorts-research.md`](01-viral-finance-shorts-research.md), which covers methodology, the top examples table, cross-cutting principles, platform rules, trends and whitespace.
**What this is:** the final ranked shortlist of short-form **formats** (mechanics, not topics). It merges three independent strategy panels and settles their disagreements against the raw evidence in `research/raw/` and `research/watch/`.
**Evidence rule:** every example below is copied from those files, with its view count, outlier score and URL. "Watched" means a scene-by-scene vidIQ breakdown exists. "Transcript" means the spoken words were pulled. "Inferred" means the format was judged from metadata only. Calculations marked "(our check)" were redone by us. All illustrative returns and rates are assumptions to be labelled on screen.

## How the ranking works

- **Expected impact for a new, zero-subscriber, faceless channel** = 0.4 × Virality + 0.3 × Replicability + 0.3 × Envelope fit, each scored 1–10.
  - **Virality:** the ceiling and consistency of reach across the evidence, weighting small-account breakouts above big-channel hits.
  - **Replicability:** how easily a faceless channel with no audience, no presenter and no licensed footage can produce it every week.
  - **Envelope fit:** how naturally the format shows a rough-but-right calculation on an envelope and supports both meanings of "envelope" (back-of-the-envelope math and cash-envelope budgeting).
- **Ranks 7–10 are within 0.2 points of each other**, which is effectively a tie. Choose among them by production capacity.

## 1. Cost in Envelopes (the Unit Swap)

**One-liner:** Restate a price or a fortune nobody can picture as a count of one familiar, cheap unit (lattes, RTX cards, Red Bulls, grains of rice, $10K envelopes) and let the count be the punchline.

**Scores:** Virality 10 · Replicability 9 · Envelope fit 9 → **9.40**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** People can't feel the gap between 10⁶ and 10⁹ (magnitude neglect). A familiar unit makes it something they can picture. **The unit choice is itself the hook.** Youth-coded or topical units (lattes, Monster, Red Bull, RTX 5090 during the 2026 GPU price spike, iPhone 17 Pro in launch week) produce an instant "wait, HOW many?" and a reason to send it. A running counter synced to physical drops works as a progress bar plus ASMR.
- **Algorithmic.** No language and probably no voice-over (the lattes Short returned "No transcript available"), so it can be shown to seed audiences in any country. Runs of 11–40 s finish easily. Ending on the full pile and cutting back to one unit makes a clean loop, and replays count as views on YouTube (since 2025-03-31) and Instagram. The title is a fill-in template ("Cost in Units of ___"), so every episode is a fresh test with a new unit.

### Beat-by-beat anatomy (observed in the winners)

### The Envelope Math build (~25 s)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–1 s | Envelope front: **"COST IN ENVELOPES: [hot item of the week]"**. Flap stamp: "1 envelope = $10,000 (source, date)" |
| 1–15 s | Envelopes thud onto a growing stack. Top badge counts up. 4–8 escalating items |
| ~15 s | The **one "so what" line** the originals lack, handwritten: "= X years of median pay" (sourced) |
| 18–22 s | Scale shot: stack beside a person, fridge or building, with physically correct height (notes × thickness) |
| last 1–2 s | Cut to a single envelope (loop). Next unit teased on the flap |

Variants: a **denomination ladder** (the same $1M in $5s vs $100s, by height or weight); **"[Person] is N× richer than [fictional character]"**; a rice-grain or pixel scale.

### Hook formulas
- Cost in Envelopes: [hot item of the week]
- Cost in Units of [cheap, recognisable, topical item]
- What $[big number] looks like in [envelopes / $100 bills / Big Macs]
- How tall is $[1 billion] in $10K envelopes?
- [Person] is [N]x richer than [fictional rich character]
- 1 [unit] = $[X]. Here's [a billionaire / the national debt / a stadium].

**Best platforms:** YouTube Shorts first (11–30 s), where all the core evidence is. Cross-post the wordless master to Reels and TikTok. Time episodes to price news and launch weeks.

**Pitfalls:**
1. **Template saturation and policy.** Three copycat channels appeared within weeks. YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule (Jul 2025) and its Oct 1 2026 Shorts update name "template-based bulk changes". Vary the structure and add our own sourced calculation and "so what" line.
2. **The unit decides everything.** Pick units with a live price story.
3. **Raw numbers without a conversion fail.**
4. **Prop cash invites "fake" debates.** The €500 note is no longer issued. Use animated, physically accurate stacks.
5. **HD Guy's on-screen content was never watched** (vidIQ tags hint at military hardware). Don't assume its visuals.
6. **Date and source every unit price.**

## 2. The Rate Clock

**One-liner:** Divide one incomprehensible sum by a human-scale rate ($1/second, $50K/hour, $4B/day) and land on a time or distance you can feel: "15 laps of the Earth", "32 years of your life", "you'd have started before Julius Caesar was born".

**Scores:** Virality 9 · Replicability 9 · Envelope fit 10 → **9.30**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** It turns money into lived time, so the payoff is about mortality, not dollars. It has **two payoffs**: an early concrete one (Story Snap's distance at about 6 s) and an emotional twist at the end, so viewers stay and then rewatch. It is a single division viewers can check in their heads, which is pure Fermi estimation. Famous mega-subjects (billionaires, the debt, "trillionaire" headlines) bring their own curiosity and polarisation. The "this guy must be lying, let me check" arc holds even 65 s of uncut calculator footage.
- **Algorithmic.** Versions of 14–17 s loop. Tilbury's math Shorts carry his highest breakouts (11.91x, above his skits at 4.69x). Timely searches ("elon becomes trillionaire") give it a seed audience. Story Snap's two-sentence Short is the most-viewed in its cluster.

### Beat-by-beat anatomy (observed)
- **Two-sentence loop (Story Snap, 14 s):** 0–2 s rate premise ("If you picked up $1 every second while walking…") → 2–5 s target ("…to reach $1 billion") → **~6 s payoff 1** ("walk around the earth 15 times") → 6–14 s **payoff 2** ("…32 years of your life are gone") → hard end and loop. (Our check: $1B ÷ $1/s ≈ 31.7 years.)
- **Fact-check variant (sog_geovanie, 107 s):** 0–34 s someone else's claim (32%) → 34–99 s skepticism plus live calculator steps, each a mini-reveal (2,059 → 1.2M → 438M) → last 8 s: $901.8B, still short.
- **Story variant (Dr Bandana, 63 s):** stakes in sentence 1 ("spend $1B in 24 hours or prison") → hour-stamped beats (Hour 1, 3, 9, 16, 23, Midnight) → "If this was you, how would you spend it?"

### The Envelope Math build (~20 s)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Dare, on screen and spoken: "Earn $50,000 an hour, every hour since Year 1, and you STILL don't have $1 trillion." |
| 2–4 s | "Check it on one envelope." Pen: $1,000,000,000,000 ÷ $50,000/hr = **20,000,000 hours** |
| 4–7 s | Payoff 1: ÷ 8,766 hrs/yr ≈ **2,280 years** (circled) |
| 7–13 s | Payoff 2: a timeline dot slides back from 2026 past Year 1 to about 250 BC: "You'd have started before Julius Caesar was born." (Our check; Caesar was born in 100 BC) |
| 13–17 s | Pre-empt the nitpick ("AD counts from the birth, and the answer doesn't change") or plant one labelled rounding for the math police |
| 17–20 s | The last line flows back into the dare (loop) |

Show the rate the story versions never compute: $1B in 24 h = **$41.7M an hour ≈ $11.6K a second** (our check).

### Hook formulas
- If you earned $[X] every second, how long until $[Y]?
- How long would it ACTUALLY take to [pick up / spend / count] $[X]?
- Earn $[X] an hour since [historic moment] and you STILL don't have [person]'s $[Y].
- Spending $[X] in [N] seconds
- How long does it take [company] to make $1 million?
- $[X] a day would still take [N] years to pay off [the national debt].

**Best platforms:** YouTube Shorts, at 14–49 s (all the core evidence). TikTok for a 60–110 s fact-check walkthrough. Reels: untested, so treat it as an experiment.

**Pitfalls:**
1. **Rate and calendar errors get torn apart.** sog_geovanie's "AD = After Death" slip drew correction comments, and a published guide mislabels per-day dollars as per-second (an 86,400x error). Mind 365 vs 365.25.
2. **Abstract or jargon framing fails.** The best "Rule of 72" Short got 29,860 views.
3. **Net worth is not cash** (Founder Files' contrarian take hit 220x). Date and source every net-worth figure.
4. **Keep inequality neutral.** It is a conversion, not a political statement.
5. **Celebrity repeats decay** (Behind the Border: 535x → 29.8x → 5.2x). Rotate subjects and rates.
6. **A slow derivation kills a 14 s loop.** One line, no more.

## 3. The Read-It Ladder

**One-liner:** A 5–8 s single shot in which one small recurring number climbs the calendar as a static text block ("$3 a day · $21 a week · $1,080 a year · $10,800 every 10 years"), capped by an anti-guilt or scale-up punchline. Reading it takes longer than the clip runs, so viewers let it loop.

**Scores:** Virality 8.5 · Replicability 10 · Envelope fit 9 → **9.10**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** Viewers map it straight onto their own habit ("my $27 is iced coffee + DoorDash"). Small × time = shock is the purest envelope move. The best versions **flip the expected lecture**: "…not enough to buy a nice car so enjoy your caffeine bro" validates the viewer, and "NOW IMAGINE HAVING 10 OF THEM 🥹" sells the dream. Both split the YOLO camp from the invest-it camp in the comments. Rounding, deliberate or not, calls in the "math police": $90×12 = $1,080 vs $3×365 = $1,095, and "$27/day = $10,000" when it's $9,855.
- **Algorithmic.** Read time ≈ runtime (6–8 s), so completion runs past 100% and replays count. Production is close to zero (a hand, a product, one text block). It is the **highest-outlier hook type in the IG/TikTok sweep**, with a daily-habit cluster median of 285.7x.

### The Envelope Math build (~8 s)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 s | Rung 1 already on the envelope beside the product ("$3 a day") |
| 0.8–4 s | Pen writes rungs 2–5 with a scratch SFX each: ≈$21/wk · ≈$90/mo · ≈$1,095/yr · ≈$10,950/decade. The "≈" marks the rounding as deliberate |

| loop | Last frame circles rung 1, which matches the first frame |

**Hustle-claim variant:** the hero stat is the payback, $3,000 ÷ $400 = **7.5 weeks**, followed by one audit line for the costs left out. That hands over to approach 10. **Extended variant (20–35 s):** a wage ladder ($X/hr → week → year → month), extended with a back-of-envelope constant such as "$1,000 borrowed at 6.5% ≈ $6.32/month" (Bran the Mortgage Man's rule, see approach 7) to reach a house price.

### Hook formulas
- A [habit] is $[X] a day.
- $[X] a day · $[7X] a week · $[365X] a year · $[3,650X] every 10 years
- When I said $[27]/day is $[10,000] a year (math police)
- Buy [thing] for $[A], rent it for $[B]/week = $[C] a year. Now imagine 10.
- A $[annual goal] is just $[goal ÷ 365] a day.
- If you make $[X]/hr full time, that's $[Y] a month.

**Best platforms:** Instagram Reels and TikTok as native 5–8 s loops (music only or no audio). YouTube Shorts is proven too (Kel King, 7 s). Export a clean master for each platform, with no foreign watermarks.

**Pitfalls:**
1. **Lecturing is the anti-pattern.** The punchline must validate or surprise.
2. **Leaving out growth or costs** is fine as a labelled hook and toxic as a brand claim. Kel King's "$19,200 made" ignores the purchase price, insurance and depreciation, and uses a 48-week year. "FIRE YOUR BOSS" brushes YouTube's get-rich-quick policy and the FCA's urgency warnings.
3. **It is the easiest format to clone.** Theme pages copy captions word for word within weeks (@moneymovesjake and @jacobhartmanofficial posted identical "$17.50 after taxes" text). The handwriting and the front/back flip are the moat.
4. **Text-heavy Reels.** Instagram advises against Reels "predominantly covered by text", so keep the block short and large.
5. **Loops lift reach, not YouTube engaged views**, and licensed trending audio cuts the Shorts revenue share.

## 4. Two Envelopes (Pick-One Dilemma → Race to the Crossover)

**One-liner:** Put two money paths side by side where intuition and math disagree: $1M now vs $1,000/week for life, $200M once vs $20/second, a doubling penny, the early starter vs the late starter. The viewer picks in second one, and the envelope runs the clock to the break-even or the overtake.

**Scores:** Virality 9 · Replicability 9 · Envelope fit 9 → **9.00**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.**
  - **Silent commitment** in 1–2 s, so the ego is invested before any explanation starts.
  - An ego challenge raises the stakes ("Most people get this WRONG").
  - The "obvious" lump sum usually loses to a rate or an exponent. Teacherman's "$1,000 per second" option is worth **$31.5B a year** (our check): the smallest-looking number wins because of its unit.
  - A tension trough keeps intuition alive (the penny is only **$163.84 on day 15**).
  - Race versions create suspense about *when*, not *whether*: Behind the Border's overtake lands at about 55% of runtime. They end on two numbers side by side.
- **Algorithmic.**
  - Dilemma cards of 5–11 s loop past 100%.
  - Comment sections fill with picks and arguments (that day's amount vs the running total, the return rate, how many years).
  - A twist re-hook ("What if it's a dollar per push-up?") restarts the debate.
  - It is the most consistent small-account breakout across all three platforms.
  - Music-only race tickers are language-free and cheap to batch.

## 5. The Envelope Split

**One-liner:** Take one specific paycheck and split it on screen into labelled envelopes by fixed ratios (55/5/10/15/15 or 50/15/5/30), with each formula turning into dollars as the cash drops. Then add the layer nobody adds: **how long each envelope takes to fill**, and what it becomes.

**Scores:** Virality 8 · Replicability 9 · Envelope fit 10 → **8.90**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** It is participatory: every viewer silently runs the ratios on their own pay. A system that is **visible but not yet explained in frame 1** creates curiosity (@moneyletter's four cups labelled 50/15/5/30 are on screen before a word is spoken). A numbered open loop ("1 of 5") shows how much is left. The emotional arc goes from **restriction to permission** ("do not spend a single dollar" … "$600 guilt-free"). Oddly specific amounts ($5,416.33) read as real and invite comparison. Cash dropping into containers is satisfying ASMR.
- **Algorithmic.** Frameworks get saved ("Save for later"). Comment-keyword DM funnels ("Comment BUDGET", 316x) and personalised-reply CTAs ("comment your take-home", 125.1x) drive comments. "Impossible in my city" sparks debate. **Paycheck cluster median 453.7x.** 13 creators posted 16 near-identical "financially free in 5 steps" clips in 90 days (about 12.7M plays), which is proof that demand outlasts saturation. Cash ASMR is the largest raw-reach IG cluster (16.3M across 4 posts).

**One-liner:** Hold one number fixed (a $1M salary, a $1M target, one iPhone, one hour of work) and change one thing per beat (country, company, job, year), ordered so the most extreme case lands last.

## 8. The Trap Card (Envelope Puzzle)

**One-liner:** A silent 5–7 s still of a short money puzzle handwritten on an envelope. It names and rules out the tempting wrong answer up front ("It's NOT back to even!") and gives no solution. The comment section is the payoff, and the answer comes in a flip-the-envelope follow-up.

**Scores:** Virality 8.5 · Replicability 10 · Envelope fit 7.5 → **8.65**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** It creates instant dissonance: the brain computes the intuitive answer, and the card has already declared it wrong. An ambiguity where both readings feel right splits viewers into camps. A permission frame ("Answer without Googling…") turns watching into a test. Viewers who got caught and viewers who feel smug both comment. Handwriting reads as native, not as an ad.
- **Algorithmic.** A still that takes longer to solve than to watch forces pauses and rewatches, so retention goes past 100%. Instagram counts repeat views. It costs almost nothing, which allows high cadence. **Riddle and money-puzzle cluster median: 174.4x.** @mathsgenius222 got about 660 views per follower, the most cost-efficient format in the scan.

## 9. The Itemized Tally (Receipts & Cost Teardowns)

**One-liner:** Walk a familiar price or a real artifact line by line (a 2005 Walmart receipt, the ingredients of a $5.29 Big Mac, the cost stack of a $7 latte, a lottery payout ladder). Keep a running total on the envelope and hold the hero number (total, margin or house edge) to the last seconds.

**Scores:** Virality 8.5 · Replicability 8 · Envelope fit 9 → **8.50**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.**
  - Each line is a "higher or lower?" micro-game, and the held total is an open loop.
  - A found artifact supplies proof and curiosity in one sentence ("I found a receipt that's over 20 years old").
  - A famous price plus a yes/no question ("Is it a rip-off or not?") forces a stance.
  - A **planned pattern break at 35–40%** resets attention: milk got cheaper, or "but there are hidden costs".
  - Two numbers side by side ($32.59 vs $73.66) share better than one.
  - Leaving the final subtraction to the viewer drove **5,127 comments** on the Big Mac.
- **Algorithmic.** Serial micro-payoffs hold completion even at 95 s (Monarch, 603x on 4.9K subs). Brand-name search reach extends beyond finance, and the title templates repeat ("Then vs Now:", "Cost vs Price:").

5. **Gambling topics hit ad-suitability limits.** Keep the framing explicitly anti-gambling, expected value only.
6. **Correct dramatic-but-wrong framings** (Matka's "99%") instead of repeating them. Inflation comparisons need sourced CPI.

## 10. The Envelope Audit (Claim Check)

**One-liner:** Paraphrase a viral money claim in second one ("I write off my private chef", "$400 a week from a $3K car", "just pay the minimum"). Credit its true part, then red-pen the missing lines on the envelope until the honest number and a verdict stamp appear.

**Scores:** Virality 8 · Replicability 7.5 · Envelope fit 10 → **8.45**

### Why it goes viral
- **Psychological.** A debunk is a built-in open loop: "is this real?" is only answered at the verdict. An outrageous claim with visible disbelief in the first 1.5 s validates the viewer's own skepticism. There is a double payoff: either the claim survives the correction (a corrected trillion calc is still under $1T) or it collapses ("a write-off is a coupon worth your tax rate"). Believers and skeptics both comment, and viewers send it to "the friend who writes everything off".
- **Algorithmic.** It drives comment velocity and reaches beyond finance (small-business owners, tax TikTok, side-hustlers). Claims are in endless supply: about 70% of graded finance TikToks scored C or lower. Meta doesn't recommend low-quality finance content, so correct, sourced math stays recommendable. Vivian Tu's breakout came from "I actually showed people the math" (3M views and 100K followers in a week, press-reported, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/01/28/how-your-rich-bff-vivian-tu-built-a-massive-tiktok-following.html)).

## Rules that apply to every approach

Full evidence for these is in report 01, §3–4.

1. **Number or dilemma in frame 1. Title = on-screen text = first spoken line.**
2. **Two length lanes, cut per platform:** 5–15 s loops and 35–155 s walkthroughs, with a new number every 2–5 s. Typical cuts: ~25 s YouTube / ~45 s Instagram / ~65 s TikTok.
3. **Early partial payoff, a pattern break at 35–40%, the hero number at 85–95%, then a loop back to frame 1.**
4. **Show the envelope:** 1–3 lines, labelled assumptions, a range where inputs are uncertain, and a postmark-style source stamp.
5. **One honest thing to argue about:** a deliberate "≈" rounding with the exact figure pinned, or a contestable assumption. Never a factual error, and no "comment YES" bait.
6. **A taggable person in every script** (Instagram's top non-follower signal is sends) and a save-worthy rule.
7. **Series identity with a rotating variable** ("Cost in Envelopes: ___", "Envelope Estimate #N", "Envelope Puzzle #N"). Same template, new subject every time. Never delete and re-upload a flop.
8. **Original everything:** our own handwriting and animation, no TV or creator clips, no foreign watermarks, original or royalty-free audio. Teach math, not picks, with no get-rich-quick framing.
