# Hook bank: how the finance-math benchmark opens its shorts

**Prepared for:** *Back of the Envelope* (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok)
**Date:** 2026-10-07
**Role:** hook analyst, round 2.
**Evidence base:** only the approved benchmark. That means the 7 finance-math-core channels (@real_unick, HD Guy, FinCalC TV, ChartOrbit, @investment_timeline, @thedebtfreedomproject, @gageheward) plus the maths series of the 3 near-miss accounts (Jake @jacobdoesmoney, The Market Hustle, Master Money). Sources: [`01-finance-math-channels.md`](01-finance-math-channels.md) and the ten benchmark studies in [`watch/`](watch/). `watch/alan-becker.md` is a look reference, not a benchmark, so nothing in this file comes from it.
**Credits:** no new vidIQ calls were made for this file (0 credits). Every hook, view count, multiple and URL is copied from the study files or from `channels.json`, the qualification data. Where none of those has a value, this file says "unknown". Any arithmetic I did for this file is labelled **(check)**.

**How to read the numbers**
- **Views** are as the study files report them on 2026-10-07. On Instagram they are plays.
- **Outlier: a bare multiple** such as `62.49x` is vidIQ's own score. On YouTube that is the breakoutScore. On Instagram and TikTok it is views ÷ the creator's median, from vidIQ's outlier index.
- **Outlier: `(6.6x med)`** is the study's own ratio of plays to that account's median. It is not a vidIQ score, and each channel's header names the median used. Don't compare the two kinds of multiple with each other.
- **Outlier: `n/a`** means no multiple was returned or computed.
- **Src** says where the hook text comes from:
  - `W`: watched scene by scene (vidIQ walkthrough).
  - `C`: read off the cover thumbnail, which may not be frame 1.
  - `T`: title or caption returned by the catalogue tool.
  - `I`: vidIQ index metadata only (the video was not watched).
  - `E`: an earlier outlier search saved in this project.
- **Slot labels:**
  - `T` is the title on YouTube, or the caption on IG and TikTok.
  - `F1` is the on-screen text at frame 1.
  - `S` is the first spoken line (0-3 s).
- **Pat** is the pattern ID from section 2. `–` means the hook fits none of the nine. Rows marked **contrast** are kept on purpose: they show what does *not* work inside the same channel.

## 0. Summary

- **91 hooks are banked from the 10 benchmark accounts, and 83 of them have a URL.** All 9 patterns use only these hooks.
- **The strongest patterns by multiple:**
  - "What difference does X make?" with a verdict caption: up to **902.5x**.
  - "Same money, two choices": up to **345.09x**, and a 15.9M-view peak.
  - "Re-price it in a unit you know": up to **183.76x**, and 44.8M views.
  - "POV: you invested in X instead of paying $Y for X": up to **140.6x**.
  - "Take your income × 0.X": **140x**, 3.0M views.
  - "Find your row": 1.15M views, or 54.46x on a 6 s card.
- **The anti-pattern is goal-first:** "How long to reach $Y?" It sits at the bottom of every one of the 4 channels that use it.
- **What the winners share:**
  - A number the viewer owns, or can swap their own into, is on screen at 0.0 s.
  - The stake is named ("you", an amount, a start date).
  - Exactly one question stays open: who wins, which row is mine, or what the real number is.
- **Our six old hooks were not too long.** They run 9-12 words, while the benchmark winners run 6-15 (**check**). They failed on content:
  - Four of six use a number the viewer doesn't own: $1 trillion, $50,000/hr, "$1,000 of pay" and a generic "habit".
  - Two either give the result away ("Still No $1 Trillion") or never pay it off ("Ours is sealed in the pin").
  - Two open on a label or an unnamed set ("Itemized:", "6 countries").
- **The best single rewrite for each old hook** (all 12 rewrites are in section 4):
  1. Hot dogs: "Elon's $1 Trillion, Split With Everyone on Earth: Your Share".
  2. $50K an hour: "$1 Trillion at Your Hourly Wage", a find-your-row table.
  3. $3 a day: "What $3 a day costs you by age".
  4. 6 countries: "Same $1,000 Paycheck, USA vs Germany: Who Keeps More?"
  5. $2,500 biweekly: "3 DEAD SIMPLE NUMBERS / IF YOU'RE PAID EVERY 2 WEEKS".
  6. Chipotle: "What You're Paying For When You Spend $10 at Chipotle:"

### P1. "Take your income × 0.X" over an empty numbered list

- **Formula:**
  - Header: "[N] DEAD SIMPLE NUMBERS [for topic]", or "Do all N [if you make $X/hr] and watch your finance change", or "How to be financially free (in N steps)".
  - Below it, empty slots "1. 2. 3. 4.".
  - Spoken: "Take your [salary / monthly income] and multiply it by [0.X]".
  - Each slot goes input → formula → result.
- **Channels: 4 of 10.**
  - Master Money, Jake, and @real_unick.
  - @gageheward appears only through an outlier search: "How to be financially free (In 4 steps)", 186.3K, 23.9x.
- **Best examples:**
  - H84 Master Money, 3,000,000 (140x), and its IG mirror at 1,700,000 (27.8x).
  - H76 Jake, 983,900 (36.3x), and the second post at 942,800 (23.2x).
  - H86 Master Money car, 554,900 (35.6x).
  - H55 Yannick, "Do all 5", 115,845 (67.1x).
- **Why it works for finance math:**
  - The constant *is* the rough-but-right rule: ×0.7 for tax, ×0.35 for the most a car should cost, ×200 for a nest-egg target.
  - The viewer runs it on their own income while watching, so the hook is participation, not a claim.
  - The empty slots show how many payoffs remain.
  - The first answer lands before 3 s ($50,400 in H84).
  - The finished list is a cheat sheet, so viewers save it.
- **Watch out:**
  - It is a trend template. 20 of the 57 other outliers in Master Money's search used "How to be financially free (in N steps)".
  - Jake's own remake (H83) did 14,232 (0.29x med).
  - The version that is not a copy is Master Money's: a branded series name and one concrete topic per episode.
  - Keep constants consistent: his 3M reel mixes 50- and 52-week years.

### P2. Small amount × time =

  - Variants:
    - "Does investing 100$ monthly in [X] make you rich?" (a yes/no verdict).
    - "You've got 93 days left in 2026." + "$X each day = $Y".
    - "How hitting $100K by age 40 and never investing again would grow".
- **Channels: 4 of 10.** FinCalC, ChartOrbit, The Market Hustle and Master Money.
- **Best examples:**

- **Why it works for finance math:**
  - Compounding is invisible in one number. A ladder (Year 1 … Year 15) or a racing line makes it visible.
  - A small, affordable amount includes everyone.
  - "1-15 years" lets each viewer stop at their own horizon.
  - The yes/no "make you rich?" version adds a verdict to wait for.
- **Watch out:**
  - FinCalC's 2026 6 s cards in the same formula stalled: 9,017 and 10,445.

- **Watch out:**
  - The 739K hook is an identity-plus-duty sticker, not a number.

### 2.10 Devices that work on top of any pattern

- **Formula, then result, in the same slot.** For example "5,000 X 200" becomes "$1M" (H55), "$72,000 * 0.7" becomes "$50,400" (H84), and "$6,000 *.55" becomes "$3,300" (H76).
- **A one-line footer holding the single assumption.** For example "Tall Latte ☕ = $4.45" (H04) or "Price of RTX 5090 32GB: ~$4,899" (H01).
- **A spoken opener that is the first calculation, with no greeting.** See H55, H57, H76, H84 and H86. Debt Freedom opens on credentials instead and needs 3-6 min to pay off.
- **The viewer's comment as the brief,** shown in the native reply sticker (H50, 127.3x). It also makes sequels cheap.
- **Open in the red.**
  - ChartOrbit's counters already sit below the stake at 0.0 s (H16, H17).

- **Identity or grievance stickers.** "Paycheck rules / That every family / Should follow" (H53) and "They Should've Taught This in School" (H55).
- **Comment-keyword CTAs lift comments, not views.**

## 3. Rules for a strong finance-math hook

Each rule comes with the benchmark evidence behind it and a quick test to run on a draft hook.

**R1. A dollar figure is on screen at 0.0 s, and it is the input or the full answer, not a teaser card.**
- **Evidence:**
  - All four watched HD Guy shorts open with the counter or the unit already running ("$0.30", "3" lattes).
  - ChartOrbit's counters are on screen at 0.0 s.
  - Master Money's slot 1 is typing "$72,00" at 0.0 s.

## 5. Caveats and unknowns

- **Two kinds of multiple.** vidIQ multiples and the studies' own "x med" ratios use different baselines. Compare within a channel, not across.
- **Unwatched hooks.**
  - @investment_timeline: no video could be watched (all 7 jobs failed). Its frame-1 text and payoffs come from vidIQ index metadata only.
  - Debt Freedom's H51 was not watched.
  - Yannick's, Gage's, Jake's, The Market Hustle's and Master Money's unwatched reels are read from covers, which may not be frame 1.
- **Recency confounds.** Several low rows are 1-11 days old: H60, H61, H63, H67 and H69 among them. P3's verdict rests partly on newer posts.
- **Pattern counts.** They cover only the reels the tools returned: 12 per IG account, 5 per TikTok account, and 93-100 per YouTube channel. A pattern can exist in a channel's back catalogue without being counted here.
- **The rewrites' inputs** (the iPhone 18 Pro price, the median home, OECD 2025 tax, Chipotle's 2025 split, 8.2 billion people) come from our own earlier specs. They were not re-checked against their sources for this file.
- **No rewrite has been tested.** Each is a hypothesis built from the patterns above.
