## Summary

- **Every breakout in the benchmark puts money on screen inside a fixed, typeset frame, and none of them looks hand-made.** Across the ten accounts there is no paper texture, collage, stamp, tape, postmark, sticky note, mascot or live handwriting. Nine of the ten set their numbers in heavy sans-serif type, and nine use a neutral field with one or two accent colours that carry meaning.
- **Beyond type and colour, seven more traits are shared by the biggest finance-math breakouts (section 2.2):**
  1. a money number visible at 0.0 s;
  2. one persistent header that states the rule;
  3. the structure visible before it fills (empty slots, blank rows, a running year);
  4. one continuous shot in which the numbers are the motion;
  5. one operation repeated down a ladder;
  6. the biggest number placed deliberately, either first or last;
  7. an ending on the finished screen, with no end card.
- **Three directions, each built from a different part of the benchmark:**
  1. **Clean Sheet** is a crisp top-down worksheet: typeset formulas, highlighter results and a pointer. It comes from Yannick's notebook sheets plus the numbered-slot device.
  2. **Live Sheet** is a designed spreadsheet on black: formula bar, rows that fill, one yellow accent. It comes from FinCalC, The Debt Freedom Project, The Market Hustle and Gage Heward.
  3. **Scoreboard** is a dark HUD: a neon money counter, stacks of a unit object, and races. It comes from HD Guy, ChartOrbit, Jake's duels and @investment_timeline.
- **Recommendation: lead with Live Sheet.** Its look family is where big reach and visible numbers-in-context meet most often in the benchmark:
  - FinCalC's row ladder puts the input beside every output: 3,771,667 views.
  - Debt Freedom's formula bar shows the actual formula: 1.9M views at 902.5x.
  - Gage's lookup table lays out every case: 1,150,974 plays.

  Its row numbers can also carry Master Money's formula-to-result slot device (3M at 140x).

  It carries all nine shared traits from section 2.2 natively, and it is the furthest of the three from the rejected look. Borrow Scoreboard's rolling counter for the final cell. Keep Scoreboard as a second lane for unit swaps and races. Park Clean Sheet.

## 3. Three look directions

### 3.0 Shared foundation (applies to all three)

- **Canvas:** 1080×1920 at 30 fps.
- **Safe zones** (the same numbers the engine linter enforces; the benchmark headers sit inside them):

| Zone | Rule |
|---|---|
| y 0-230 | Platform top bar. Decoration only (brand mark). |
| y 240-440 | **Header band.** The rule or question lives here in every direction. Benchmark headers sit at about 8-25% of the height (Master Money 232-353 px measured). |
| y 440-1300 | Main working area |
| y 1320-1480 | Caption band (when captions run) or the label/footer band |
| y 1480-1920 | Platform caption and UI. Nothing readable. |
| x | Left margin ≥ 60. Readable content at x ≤ 1020 above y 820, and at x ≤ 940 below y 820 (right button rail). |

- **Type floor:** primary text 60-90 px. That matches the benchmark: Master Money's list is about 61 px, his header 68-84 px and his captions 74 px; Debt Freedom's question is about 80 px. Anything the viewer must read: at least 40 px. Secondary labels that repeat elsewhere: at least 34 px.
- **Numbers:** tabular figures (no jitter while counting), comma grouping, "$" prefix, units written out ("/mo", "a year"). **"≈" marks every rounded result.** That is the brand's honesty device (see the motif rules below).
- **One assumption line** whenever a number rests on an assumption, visible from frame 1, as in HD Guy's footer and FinCalC's rate in the title.
- **Banned in all three directions:** kraft or paper texture, grain, collage, tape, stamps, postmarks, wax seals, sticky notes, handwriting fonts, mascots, calculator keypads, intros, end cards.
- **What survives from the envelope brand (at most):** a small line-icon envelope mark (46-56 px, one colour, in the decoration zone) and the "≈" sign used as the operator for rounded answers. The channel name stays. Nothing else carries over.

**How a calculation appears, step by step**
1. Each rung is one operation on the same input: × 7 days, × 365, × 10 years.
2. On each rung the counter resets and rolls up to the new total (0.6 s ease-out), just as HD Guy resets per item.
3. Icons drop in to the new count (0.25 s each, with a small bounce). As the count grows the grid gets denser, until icons become dots that fill the stage.
4. The footer rewrites to that rung's one-line maths.
5. Race mode follows: the same money split two ways, as two lines from zero with tip counters, the axis rescaling as values grow. Real-data races may add crash bands; a fixed-rate hypothetical must not, because it has no crashes.
6. The biggest number lands last in the top-bar counter.

## 4. Recommendation: lead with Live Sheet

**Why Live Sheet:**

1. **It is the look family where big reach and visible working meet most often in the benchmark.**
   - FinCalC's row ladder puts the input beside every output: 3,771,667 views.
   - Debt Freedom's spreadsheet shows the actual formula in the formula bar: 1.9M at 902.5x.
   - Gage's lookup table lays out every case at once: 1,150,974 plays.
   - The sheet's row numbers carry Master Money's formula → result slots (3M at 140x) without needing his face or studio.

   The Scoreboard family reaches further, but it shows only results. The qualification file names the opening for this channel as a faceless look with visible rough-but-right working, and the formula bar plus the cells show exactly that.
2. **It carries all nine shared traits from section 2.2 by construction:**
   - A money answer in row 2 at 0.0 s (W1).
   - A persistent banner header (W2).
   - Row numbers with empty output cells, so the structure is visible (W3).
   - Heavy sans with tabular figures (W4).
   - One yellow accent with green and red results (W5).
   - One continuous shot in which the cells are the motion (W6).
   - One operation per column, filled at a steady beat (W7).
   - The biggest number placed by row order (W8).
   - An ending on the finished table that loops (W9).
3. **It covers both proven lengths and most of the benchmark's winning formats in one template:**
   - the 6 s find-your-row card (FinCalC 54.46x and 51.68x; Gage's 14 s table);
   - the 20-40 s walkthrough (FinCalC's 30 s ladder; Master Money's input → result swap);
   - the "what difference does X make" comparison (Debt Freedom).

   A single fixed grid that only changes its content is what Master Money does, down to the pixel.
4. **It is the furthest from the rejected look.** There is no paper and no pen. The envelope survives only as "≈" in the formula bar, where the rough-but-right promise actually lives.
5. **It is the easiest to keep correct and consistent.** Every cell is computed, so the screen cannot drift from the maths. That avoids the benchmark's own mistakes: FinCalC's "12%" label on a 10% table, Master Money's 50-week vs 52-week seam, and Jake's caption vs screen mismatch.

**What to borrow:**
- **From Scoreboard:** the rolling count-up for the final cell, and the duel idea of two output columns filling at the same time for A-vs-B episodes.
- **From Clean Sheet:** the black input → output badge, as a variant of the banner when an episode is one input to one goal ("$20/HR → $10K?").

**What to test first** (on the same calculation, so only one variable changes):
1. **Frame 1 with row 2 filled vs all outputs empty.** The only benchmark data point is Gage's $500k pair, where the filled-table cover got 4,348 plays against 2,844 for the empty one (n = 1).
2. **The 6 s card vs the 25 s walkthrough** of the same table. FinCalC's cards bought views (13,177 median vs 2,098) but likes fell to 0.20%.
3. **Live Sheet vs Scoreboard** on one unit-swap topic, to decide whether Scoreboard earns a second lane.

**Why not the others (yet):**
- **Scoreboard:** the strongest reach in the benchmark rests on footage we cannot use (HD Guy) or on real market data plus brand logos (ChartOrbit). Neither shows working, and ChartOrbit collapsed when it drifted off its premise. Revisit it as the second lane for unit swaps and races once Live Sheet is running.
- **Clean Sheet:** it is the closest to what the user rejected. The benchmark's own step-by-step paper series underperformed the same creator's finished sheet (6.7K-28.9K vs 739K). Park it.

**What it means for the engine** (`engine/`): the kraft theme, tape, stamps, postmarks and handwriting stop being defaults. Live Sheet needs:
- a dark theme;
- a `banner` op;
- a `sheet` op (grid, header fills, row numbers);
- a `formulaBar` op;
- a `select` op (moving cell outline);
- a `cell` op (formula → result snap, plus an optional count-up through the existing `counter` op);
- a row-highlight op.

The existing safe-zone linter, captions and loop handling carry over unchanged.
