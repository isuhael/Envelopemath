## 1. How viability was scored

Viability (1-10) is a judgement, not a formula. It answers one question, using only the benchmark: **how likely is this format to work for a brand-new channel?** Four things were weighed:

1. **Evidence.**
   - How many benchmark accounts run the format?
   - Was it watched?
   - How big are the breakouts, and the medians behind them?
   - Is the evidence recent?
2. **Replicable by us.**
   - Can a channel with no audience make it every week?
   - The constraints: a recorded voice-over, no need for a face on camera, and no licensed footage or music. Voice is not a constraint; face is, because qualification ranked channels for faceless or low-face fit.
3. **Rough-but-right fit.** Does the mechanic show a calculation a viewer could redo on a napkin?
4. **Risks the benchmark itself shows:**
   - saturation;
   - a channel's own decline;
   - engagement collapsing;
   - numbers that do not reconcile.

Each format's score carries its reasons.

### Rank 1. Numbered rough-numbers list ("N dead simple numbers"): viability 9

**Mechanic.** One example income sits in slot 1 of an empty numbered list.
- Each slot types a formula with one round constant (`× 0.7`, `÷ 2,000`, `× 0.35`), then swaps the formula for the answer with its unit (`$50,400`, `$36/hr`, `$350/m`).
- The finished screen is a cheat sheet of N answers on one person's numbers.
- Hook pattern **P1**.

- **Pace and edit:** one rule every 4-7 s, 0 cuts. Karaoke captions sit at about 71% of frame height.
- **The biggest number on screen is the input,** shown at 0.0 s. Every later number is smaller.

**Variants seen**
- **Biggest-first (Yannick, "Do all 5"):**
  - Slot 1 is `5,000 X 200` → `$1M` at 0:02-0:08.
  - Then `× 6`, `× 0.20`, `× 0.60` and `× 0.10` on the same $5,000.
- **Wage-filtered, with the goal held back (Yannick, "$20/hr"):**
  - A badge, `$20/HR → $10K SAVED`, makes the promise at 0.0 s.
  - Four `$2,600 x .40`-style slots fill from 0:02 to 0:21.
  - The badge pays off at 0:31-0:36 with "10K saved in 3 years".
  - The list then clears to blank, so the reel loops.
- **Whole budget (Jake):** five slots of `$6,000 *.55` and so on, which sum to 100% of $6,000. Spoken close: "Save this for your next paycheck."

**Hook formula**
- **Header:** "[N] [SERIES NAME] NUMBERS / [for topic | That Tell You What You Actually X]", or "Do all [N] if you make $[X]/hr and watch your finance change".
- **Frame 1:** the empty slots, with slot 1 already typing the viewer's kind of number.
- **First spoken line:** "Take your [salary / monthly income] and multiply it by [0.X]." No greeting, no "did you know".

**Typical length:** 27-44 s in the benchmark. Target: about 30 s with 4 slots.

**Back-of-the-envelope twist**
- **Keep what works:**
  - the first answer within 3 s;
  - the empty slots visible at 0.0 s;
  - one example input reused across episodes (Master Money uses $72,000 in both);
  - one concrete topic per episode;
  - a series name of our own. In Master Money's search, 20 of the other 57 outliers used the generic "How to be financially free (in N steps)", and Jake's remake of it got 14,232.
- **Add:**
  1. **Keep every constant consistent across slots.**
     - Master Money's 3M reel uses 2,000 hours (50 weeks) in step 2 but $72,000 ÷ 52 weeks in step 4. So the "20% less" shows as $27.70 an hour instead of $28.80 (check).
     - His car episode assumes $5,000 take-home on $72,000. His own ×0.7 rule gives $4,200 (check).
     - Our fix: one year = 50 weeks × 40 h = 2,000 h, and one tax constant per series.
  2. **Mark each constant as rough and say where it comes from, in one footer line,** for example "×0.7 ≈ what's left after tax, rough". This borrows HD Guy's footer device ("Tall Latte ☕ = $4.45"), which shows the single input that makes the maths checkable.
  3. **End on a check slot that ties back to the input:** "= $6,000 ✓". Jake's five slots sum to 100%. Yannick's "Do all 5" covers only 90% of income, and the other 10% is never explained (check).
  4. **Declare the rounding.** Yannick's "10K saved in 3 years" is $260 × 36 = $9,360 (check). Write "≈ $9,400, call it $10K".
- **Don't:**
  - add an intro;
  - explain a constant before its result appears;
  - remove the empty slots.
- **Faceless caution:**
  - Every benchmark winner in this format has a presenter on screen.
  - The one faceless version, Jake's remake over car b-roll, got 14,232 (0.29x med). That is n = 1, and it is confounded: it remade a saturated trend.
  - So the typed list has to carry the frame on its own. Test faceless against low-face.

**Why 9:**
- **For:**
  - It is the purest rough-but-right mechanic in the set: the constant *is* the rule of thumb.
  - Four benchmark accounts run it. Five of its entries were watched: both Master Money episodes, Jake's, and two of Yannick's.
  - Its peak is 140x.
  - It is cheap to make, and the recorded voice-over is its engine.
- **Against:** the template is saturated, and the faceless version is untested.

**Hook formula**
- "What $[tiny amount] costs you by [age / wage]", or "[Payout noun] using [vehicle] at [rate]%".
- The payout noun matters. Among FinCalC's 6 s cards, payout-first titles have a median of 264,377 (n=3), against 18,835 for amount-first and 12,314 for goal-first.
- The caption gives the task ("Find your age").

**Typical length:** 5-14 s.

**Back-of-the-envelope twist**
- **Keep what works:**
  - **No talking over the table.** Gage's talked-through tables get 1,456-6,616; his silent one got 1.15M. Master Money's talked "$15 lunch by age" grid got 11,745.
  - A table denser than one pass.
  - One row per kind of viewer.
  - A cost or payout noun in the title.
  - A loop.
- **Add:**
  1. **Round every cell and state the one assumption once, in a footer.** For example "≈" values and "10% a year, before inflation". Gage puts his 10% only in the caption.   3. **Use a cover that shows the completed table.** Gage's one cover test went 4,348 for completed against 2,844 for empty (n = 1).
- **Don't:**
  - add a voice-over walkthrough;
  - cut the table to 5-6 rows;
  - turn it into a goal question.
- **Engagement warning:**
  - Like rates run 0.11% to 0.70% (FinCalC cards 0.15% and 0.11%, Gage 0.70%, Yannick's loop 0.38%).
  - The keyword-comment CTAs drew 8 and 4 comments on 428,862 and 264,377 views.
  - This format buys reach, not community.

**Why 8:**
- **For:**
  - Five benchmark accounts run it.
  - It is proven faceless (FinCalC, Yannick, Market Hustle).
  - It is the cheapest format here.
  - It holds two 50x+ breakouts and a 1.15M reel.
- **Against:**
  - Engagement is very low.
  - FinCalC's cards fell to a 13,177 median once every post was a card, and both breakouts came in the format's first four days.

### Rank 3. "What difference does X make?": one debt (or one pot of money) handled 2-4 ways: viability 8

**Mechanic.** Hold one real debt, or one pot of money, fixed.
- Work out 2-4 behaviours: monthly vs biweekly vs weekly; $50 a day vs one $1,500 lump; buying once vs monthly DCA.
- Give every option the same two outputs: total interest and a **payoff date** (or years to recover).
- Frame 1 asks the question with the options named, and the caption answers with a verdict.
- Hook pattern **P5**.

**Hook formula**

## 5. Caveats and unknowns

- **Small samples everywhere.** For each account, the studies saw these windows:
  - Instagram accounts: the 12 reels the tool returns.
  - Debt Freedom: 5 videos, all from a 4-week window.
  - @investment_timeline: 5 videos, none watched.
  - The near-miss accounts: a series each, not a channel.
- **Recency confounds.** Several Instagram comparisons set reels days old against reels months old (Yannick's wage series, Gage's $25M, Yannick's sequels).
- **Instagram multiples are mostly the studies' own medians,** not vidIQ scores. Do not compare them with YouTube breakoutScores.
- **Trend waves.**
  - The numbered list (rank 1) rode a template that 13 creators posted 16 times in one 90-day window.
  - Debt Freedom rode #micropayments.
- **None of these formats has been tested faceless on our channel.**
  - **Presenter in every watched winner:** ranks 1, 3 and 7. Rank 3 also has FinCalC's two 58-59 s loan Shorts, which were not watched, so their look is unknown.
  - **Faceless winners:** ranks 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10.
    - Rank 5: Yannick's 739K sheet shows only a hand and a pen.
    - Rank 9: the 2.14M Sukanya short has a talking-head wrapper, while the 3.77M SIP short is faceless.
- **Numbers that do not reconcile in the benchmark itself:**
  - Jake's screen vs caption finals.
  - FinCalC's 12% label over a 10% table.
  - Master Money's 50 vs 52 weeks.
  - Debt Freedom's two baseline interest figures for the same loan ($8,304.88 vs $8,192.41).

  Each is a reason for the "≈ plus one stated assumption" rule in every twist above. None is evidence that the errors hurt or helped.
- **Unknowns that could change a rank:**
