# Finance-math channels: the benchmark set (round 2, channel-first)

**Prepared for:** *Back of the Envelope*, a new short-form channel (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok) about quick, rough-but-right money calculations.
**Research date:** 2026-10-07
**Replaces:** the topic-first sampling in [`../01-viral-finance-shorts-research.md`](../01-viral-finance-shorts-research.md). That round was rejected because most of the viral videos it sampled came from channels whose core is not finance math (meme pages, a real-estate agent, a car flipper, riddle channels, a sponsored game show).
**Rule used throughout:** every view count, outlier multiple, follower count and URL below comes from the vidIQ, Instagram-reel or web data gathered in the qualification step. "n/a" means the tool gave no outlier multiple; it does not mean zero. Anything estimated is marked "est.".

---

## Summary

- **48 accounts (45 creators; three appear on two platforms) were qualified one channel at a time. 7 pass as finance-math-core, 20 are partial and 21 are rejected.**
- **The benchmark set contains only the 7 core channels.** Three partial channels pass the "their maths shorts are the ones that break out" test, but none of them is finance-math at the core. Adding them would bring back the problem this round is meant to fix, so they sit in a separate list of series-level references ([section 5](#5-series-level-references-partial-channels-not-benchmarks)).
- **Top three:** **@real_unick** (Instagram: all 12 of the reels checked are money maths, and he shows the working), **HD Guy** (YouTube: 83% cost maths, faceless, breakouts up to 30.6M views and 184x) and **FinCalC TV** (YouTube: 85% calculator maths, faceless, and the only channel that calls itself "financial math").
- **The famous finance creators fail the test.** Humphrey Yang scores 26% on YouTube and 7% on TikTok, Mark Tilbury 23%, Finance With Sharan 13% and Brian Feroldi 23%. Their maths series are format references at most.
- **TikTok evidence is the weakest part.** TikTok has no profile tool, so the two TikTok benchmarks rest on 2 videos (@investment_timeline) and 4 videos (@thedebtfreedomproject). Someone should scroll both profile grids by hand before relying on them.
- **The gap:** no channel in this pool has a large audience, a personal-finance focus and visible working all at once. HD Guy has the reach but teaches no method. @real_unick shows the working but has 15.8K followers. FinCalC shows numbers but is India-only.

## 1. Definition used

A **finance-math-core channel** is a creator whose **channel identity** (name, bio or description, recurring series) is about money numbers, **and** where **at least 60%** of their recent and popular shorts make a money calculation or a financial number the core of the video.

**Counts:** compound interest and investing-return math; money or wealth visualisations built on real numbers; budgeting and paycheck math; salary, tax or hourly-wage math; loan, APR, mortgage or credit-card math; cost breakdowns and unit economics; "how much would X be worth" calculations; retirement or FIRE math; finance educators whose shorts revolve around working numbers out.

**Does not count, even if one short went viral with money in it:** meme or entertainment pages, comedy skits, real-estate agents, car flippers or dealers, general trivia, riddle or IQ channels, motivational or "rich mindset" pages, news outlets, podcast-clip or interview channels, lifestyle vlogs, celebrity gossip, sponsored or ad posts, theme pages that repost others, and pure stock-picking or trading-signal channels (no maths taught).

**How a single short was scored:** it counts as finance math when the calculation or the number is the point of the video, not decoration. These were scored as **not** finance math: tier lists, product or ETF picks, concept explainers that work out no numbers, personal spending or net-worth diaries, and paid posts.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Discovery (channel-first)

- **YouTube:** three vidIQ channel searches across finance and money-math niches (140 channel results), Shorts searches by concept (for example "compound interest if you invest per month"), and lookups around the round-1 creators (Mark Tilbury, Humphrey Yang, Brian Feroldi).
- **Instagram and TikTok:** vidIQ's IG/TikTok outlier search by finance-math concept and by hook, plus Instagram account lookups.
- **Second wave:** accounts that turned up as side findings while other candidates were being qualified, for example @mastermoneyco, @fireby38 and @gageheward.

### 2.2 Qualification

Each of the 48 accounts was checked against both parts of the definition: identity, and the share of finance-math shorts in its catalogue.

| Platform | Accounts | How the catalogue was checked |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 16 | vidIQ catalogue: the 50 most popular plus the 50 most recent Shorts, deduplicated. That gave 21 to 100 unique Shorts per channel; Finance Invests had none. |
| Instagram | 14 | vidIQ IG profile reels: the 12 the tool returns (pinned plus newest), plus any vidIQ outlier from the same account. 12 to 13 reels per account. |
| TikTok | 18 | No profile tool exists. vidIQ TikTok outlier searches (1 to 5 videos per account) were used, plus the creator's Instagram reels as a mirror where the same videos are cross-posted. **Every TikTok share is an estimate.** |

**Verdicts:**

- **Core:** the identity is about money numbers **and** 60% or more of the checked shorts are finance math.
- **Partial:** about 30% to 59%, or a sample too small or too biased to test. The usual case was 1 to 5 videos found only by maths-seeking searches.
- **Reject:** under about 30%, or an identity in an excluded category (deals and hacks, lifestyle vlog, podcast clips, reposts, a lender funnel), whatever the share.

### 2.3 Ranking

Core channels were ranked by how useful they are as a model for a **new faceless or low-face finance-math channel**. Five things were weighed:

1. **Finance-math share.**
2. **Breakouts:** views and outlier multiples.
3. **Consistency:** typical views relative to channel size, and whether the recent feed is still maths.
4. **Closeness to rough-but-right money calculations:** relatable inputs, round numbers, working shown on screen.
5. **Faceless fit.**

The grid in [section 4](#4-scoring-grid) shows how each channel scored.

Partial channels were considered only if their finance-math shorts are the ones that break out. Three pass that test (Jake / @jacobdoesmoney, The Market Hustle and Master Money), and MoneyCoachDave Shorts comes close. All four were kept out of the ranked set because none of them is finance-math at the core. They are listed in section 5.

### 2.4 Limits

- **No video in this round was watched scene by scene.** The looks of the YouTube channels are inferred from titles, durations and vidIQ niche tags. The Instagram and TikTok looks come from the tools' reel data and descriptions.
- **TikTok shares are estimates.** See the "Evidence" column in section 4.
- **What the outlier multiples mean.** On YouTube the multiple is vidIQ's breakoutScore: views ÷ the channel's typical Short. On Instagram and TikTok it is plays ÷ the creator's median. Most reels returned by the Instagram profile tool have no multiple, so they show "n/a".
- **Time windows.** The IG/TikTok outlier index covers roughly the last 90 to 120 days. View counts were captured in the days up to 2026-10-07.

### What the seven have in common

- **Every breakout title gives a concrete input and promises a number.** Examples: "$20/HR -> $100K?!", "What If You Invested $5,000 in USA and EUROPE?" (345x), "POV: You invested in Monster instead of paying $3/day for a Monster Energy" (140.6x), "Monthly Income using Post Office MIS Scheme at 7.4% Interest Rate" (54x), "Cost in Units of Starbucks Lattes" (106x), "Look what a difference rounding makes!" (902.5x).
- **The input is either everyday or spectacular, never abstract.** Everyday means an hourly wage, a $3-a-day habit, a latte, a $5,000 paycheck or a car payment. Spectacular means an F-16 or a nuclear weapon.
- **Four of the seven are fully faceless:** CGI stacks, calculator cards and line-chart races. Two more are low-face: a notebook with a pen for half of @real_unick's reels, and a spreadsheet split screen. Only @gageheward is fully on camera.
- **Two lengths work.** One is a 6-second single-result card or loop: FinCalC's two 6 s cards reached 54x and 52x, and @real_unick's 6 s infographic reached 142.8K. The other is a walkthrough or race of about 30 to 60 s.
- **Showing the working is rare.** Only @real_unick and @thedebtfreedomproject do it consistently, and @gageheward does it partly. The others show only the result. This matches the round-1 finding that almost nobody shows the maths.
