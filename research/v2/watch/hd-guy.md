## 0. Headline findings

1. **No voice at all.** All three transcript calls returned "No transcript available", and all four watch analyses report no voice-over, only raw diegetic sound (gunfire, jet engine). The hook is 100% visual plus the YouTube title.
2. **Correction to the qualification note.** The look is *not* 3D/CGI scale renders. In all four videos watched, the picture is **real live-action military footage (16:9) letterboxed into 9:16 between solid black bars**. The only graphics are text, a neon-green money counter and flat cut-out PNGs of the unit item (GPU cards, Starbucks cups) that stack over the footage.
3. **One visual system, two hook mechanics.**
   - **Counter-only** (F-16, Rifle to Nuke): frame 1 shows nothing but a green dollar counter in the top black bar ("$0.30", "$0.00") and the action already moving. The title lives only in the YouTube title.
   - **Title-as-header** ("Cost in Units of X"): frame 1 prints the rule in the top bar ("Cost in Units of / Starbucks Lattes:") with the unit counter already running ("3", "× 1").
4. **The maths is one division, done for you on screen.** In "Cost in Units of X", the unit counter is exactly floor(cost ÷ unit price). Check: RTX video, every count matches $cost ÷ $4,899 rounded down (Javelin $127,341 → 25, Tomahawk $2,540,615 → 518, SM-3 $28,686,000 → 5,855). The unit price is always visible as a small footer legend ("Price of RTX 5090 32GB: ~$4,899", "Tall Latte = $4.45"). The counter resets with each new clip.
5. **Structure is always an escalation ladder:** start at a cheap, relatable anchor (36 cents for a rifle round, 1 GPU, 3 lattes), climb through 8-13 rungs on hard cuts every ~3 s, and end on the biggest number with no CTA. The cut from the biggest number back to the smallest gives a loop.
6. **The series that is scaling now is "Cost in Units of X"** (24 uploads since 2026-07-26). It produced the 30.6M RTX 5090 and 9.9M Starbucks breakouts, but its median is about 126K. The unit chosen decides the result: gamer and everyday-habit units (RTX 5090, Starbucks lattes, Monster Energy, Big Macs) broke out, while luxury or abstract units (Gucci, gold bars, Lamborghini, MacBook Pro, university degrees) stayed under 65K.

## 2. The HD Guy look (synthesis across the 4 watched videos)

**Frame layout (9:16)**
- **Top black bar (~20% of the height):** the money element. It is either a big neon-green dollar counter ("$0.00") or a header in two lines: "Cost in Units of" in white and "[Unit]:" in neon green (RTX) or all green (Starbucks), with the unit counter below it ("× 1" or "3" in white).
- **Middle band:** 16:9 live-action documentary footage (ship deck guns, tanks, jets, missile launches). There is a faint "HD Guy" watermark at the upper centre (seen in #3). Unit-item cut-outs (GPU cards, Starbucks cups) drop into and stack over the upper part of the footage as pyramids or grids, and fill the whole screen at the climax.
- **Bottom black bar (~20%):** a label stack, in this order:
  - the cost per shot in bold green with a dark outline ("$6,466/Round", "$5.92");
  - the item's calibre or class in big heavy white uppercase ("57 MM", "5.56 MM");
  - a descriptor in white title case ("\"3P\" Programmable Rounds", "M4 Carbine");
  - in the units series only, a small white footer legend with the unit price ("Price of RTX 5090 32GB [icon]: ~$4,899", "Tall Latte ☕ = $4.45").

**Type:** heavy geometric/display sans (the watch tool compares it to Impact, Montserrat Black and Futura), with black outlines or drop shadows. Caps for calibres, title case for names. One exception: the 2024 F-16 counter was reported as a **lime-green serif (Times New Roman-like)**, medium-bold. This looks like an older style; the 2025-26 videos use the heavy sans.

**Palette:** black bars, white labels and one neon/lime green for money (the watch tool reports #00FF00, #00FF44, #00FF66 and NVIDIA's #76B900). The footage supplies every other colour (navy grey, sea blue, desert tan, orange afterburner flame).

**How numbers appear:**
- **A rolling digital counter** that ticks up in real time with cents (F-16: $0.30 → $247.00 over ~29 s, about $8.50/s by my check) or rolls up to each item's per-shot cost (Rifle to Nuke).
- **A unit counter plus physical stacks** (units series): "× N" or "N" in the header, synced to each shot. It resets for each new item and lands on floor(cost ÷ unit price).
- **A static per-item price badge** in the bottom bar.
- None of the following ever appear: handwriting, a calculator UI, tables, spreadsheets, a formula on screen or a narrator.

**Motion and edit:** hard jump cuts timed to the firing, roughly every 3-4 s. The watch tool counted 2.7 cuts per 10 s in #1, 3.3 in #3 and 3-4 in #4. #2 is a single continuous telephoto take with 0 cuts. The stacks spawn or drop the moment a weapon fires.

**Sound:** raw, bass-heavy diegetic firing and engine sound. #4 is reported as having no music. No voice-over in any of the four.

**Length:** 26-40 s for the four studied; the catalogue spans 6 s to 1 min 28 s.

**CTA:** none in any of the four. Every video ends at peak spectacle on the biggest number.

## 5. What this means for Back of the Envelope (from this benchmark only)

What transfers:
1. **Title = the rule; frame 1 = the rule already running.** No intro and no question. The counter is mid-count at 0.0 s ("3" lattes, "$0.30").
2. **One fixed screen grammar the viewer learns once:**
   - money in the top bar (one neon green on black);
   - the subject in the middle band;
   - the label stack in the bottom bar ("$X/unit", a big caps name, a descriptor);
   - the input assumption as a tiny footer ("Tall Latte = $4.45").
   That footer is the "back of the envelope": it shows the one input that makes the maths checkable and arguable.
3. **One operation, repeated on a ladder.** Each beat is the same division (cost ÷ unit price, floored) or the same rate × time. The ladder runs from a cheap anchor to a huge finale over 8-13 rungs at about 3 s each. The biggest number always lands last.
4. **Make the number physical.** Counters plus stacks of a recognisable object do the explaining; no formula is ever shown.
5. **Pick the unit for a tribe and keep it cheap.** RTX 5090, Starbucks latte, Monster and Big Mac broke out; luxury units did not.
6. **End on the peak with no CTA; loop to rung 1.** Comments come from arguing about the inputs (GPU MSRP against street price, per-round prices).

What does not transfer:
- The footage is real military/aerospace B-roll. That spectacle is a large part of why it works, and it has nothing to do with personal finance.
- HD Guy teaches no method. The viewer never sees the working, only the result.
- The voice-free approach works because the footage carries the sound. With a recorded voice-over, the visual grammar can stay the same.
