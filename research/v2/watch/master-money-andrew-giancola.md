## 0. Headline findings

1. **The series is a fill-in-the-blanks list worked on one example salary.** Both TikTok breakouts use the same template, down to the pixel grid:
   - A 2-line header at 11 to 18% of frame height: line 1 is **"4 DEAD SIMPLE NUMBERS"** in an accent colour, line 2 is the topic in white.
   - An empty numbered list **"1. 2. 3. 4."** runs down the left 8 to 48% of the frame, at about 32%, 43%, 53% and 64% of its height.
   - Each slot types out a formula and then resolves to the answer: `1. $72,000 * 0.7` becomes `1. $50,400`.
   - Karaoke captions sit at about 71% height, with the word being spoken in the same accent colour.
   - The presenter is right of centre behind a podcast mic, in a dark bokeh studio.
   - The whole video is one take: **0 cuts**, 27.5 s and 31.2 s long.
2. **Both breakouts use the same input, $72,000 a year.** The paycheck episode turns $72,000 into $50,400 take-home, then $36/hr, $25.20/hr and $27.70/hr. The car episode turns the same $72,000 into a $25,200 maximum price, then $350/m, 2030 and $12,600.
   - **The biggest number is the input**, typed in slot 1 at 0.0 s. Every later number is smaller. The story is "shrink your salary to the truth".
3. **The hook is the first instruction, not a tease.** The spoken opener is a command with a multiplier, with no intro or "did you know":
   - *"Take your salary and multiply it by 0.7..."* (3M views)
   - *"Take your annual income and multiply it by 0.35..."* (554.9K)
   - The header supplies the promise (a number of items, "dead simple", the topic), and the voice starts the first calculation at the same moment. **The first payoff ($50,400 or $25,200) lands inside 3 seconds.**
4. **"Multiply by 0.X" is the core device.** Every step is one round constant applied to your own number: x0.7 for tax, /2,000 for hours, x0.35 for a car's price, x0.07 for a monthly payment, +4 years for a loan, x0.5 for 5-year depreciation.
   - Viewers can run each rule on their own salary straight away. This is the "rough but right" brief in its purest form, and it is what makes the reel worth saving.
5. **The series is the breakout on every platform where it was seen.** Of the 4 known episodes:
   - TikTok paycheck: **3,000,000 views at 140.0x** his 21.4K median.
   - IG paycheck mirror: **1.7M at 27.8x** a 62.8K median.
   - TikTok car: **554.9K at 35.6x** a 15.6K median.
   - IG "5 dead simple numbers to see what you can spend": **35.5K**, 3.3x the median of the 12 newest reels (10,825) and 2nd of the 12 (check).
   - The other maths formats in the feed do not break out: the "$15 lunch by age" grid got 11.7K, "$24,500 could become $10.8 million" 15.3K, "missing the 10 best days" 2.9K, and the bonus jars 7.5K.
   - The top reel in the recent IG feed (150.4K) is a non-maths car-brand tier list.
6. **The template is not his invention.** In the same outlier search, 20 of the other 57 results carry the hook text "How to be financially free (in 4/5 steps)" or "4 steps to (being) financially free". The TikTok ones decode to 2026-07-21 to 2026-08-29, so the wave was already running when his episodes went up (Aug 27 and Sep 2). Examples: @financebronextdoor 3.7M at 2,994.7x; @jacobdoesmoney2 2.8M at 124.6x; @jordfinance 1.7M at 49.9x; @gageheward on IG, "Four numbers that tell you exactly where you stand", 186.3K at 23.9x.
   - **What he added:** a branded series name ("DEAD SIMPLE NUMBERS"), one specific topic per episode (what you make, buying a car, what you can spend), and a studio look.
7. **The CTA does not explain the breakout.** The 3M paycheck reel ends with a plain *"Follow for more personal finance, investing, and wealth building tips"*. The 554.9K car reel ends with a keyword DM hook (*"Comment MMA down below..."*). Comment counts are unknown for both TikToks.
8. **There is a rough-maths seam in the 3M reel (check).** Step 2 uses 2,000 hours a year, which is 50 weeks. Step 4 uses $1,385 a week, which is $72,000 / 52 weeks. That is why step 4 gives $27.70/hr and not the $28.80 that a consistent "20% less" would give. The spoken insight, *"Most people make 20% less per hour than they actually think"*, matches the 40/50 hours ratio. The reported $27.70 is 23% below $36.
   - **Lesson for us:** keep every constant consistent across steps.
   - Caveat: this comes from vidIQ's reading of the on-screen numbers, not from my own frame-by-frame check.

## 7. Look profile (the finance-maths series only)

- **Frame:** 9:16, one locked-off medium close-up. The presenter is right of centre at a dark wood desk, hands clasped. A broadcast podcast mic sits at the right edge. The left 8 to 48% of the frame is kept clear for the list.
- **Set:** a dark studio with vertical slat walls, warm globe or Edison bulbs out of focus, and plants on both sides. It is low-key and warm, with a shallow depth of field. A secondary bright kitchen set is used only for non-maths reels (tip, scam).
- **Type:** one heavy geometric sans (vidIQ suggests Montserrat, Proxima Nova or TheBoldFont) everywhere, with a soft drop shadow and no boxes behind the header or list.
  - **Header (11 to 18% height):** 2 lines. Line 1 is the series name in an accent colour, upper case. Line 2 is the topic in white, upper or title case.
  - **List (32 to 64% height):** "1." to "4." in white bold, evenly spaced about 10% of frame height apart, shown empty from frame 1.
  - **Captions (about 71% height):** 2 to 4 word chunks, centred, upper case in the TikTok episodes. The spoken word turns the accent colour.
- **Palette:** near-black and warm brown background, white text, **one accent per episode** (neon pink #FF4DD2 approx., electric blue, yellow). Nothing else is coloured.
- **How numbers appear:** typed inline inside the list slot, in three states: input → `input * constant` (or `/`, `+`) → result with a unit (`$36/hr`, `$350/m`, `2030`). The result replaces the formula in the same slot, so the finished list is a cheat sheet of 4 answers. There are no charts, no counters and no calculator UI.
- **Motion:** 0 cuts. All movement is the typing in the list and the karaoke captions. A typing or whoosh SFX on reveals is reported by one walkthrough but contradicted by the index (unknown).
- **Length:** 27 to 31 s. Each of the 4 rules takes about 4 to 7 s.

## 8. Hook profile (the finance-maths series only)

- **Frame 1 does three jobs at once:**
  - The header states the count, the promise and the topic ("4 DEAD SIMPLE NUMBERS / FOR BUYING A CAR").
  - The empty "2. 3. 4." slots show how long the video is and tease what is coming.
  - Slot 1 is already typing the viewer's kind of number ($72,000).
- **The spoken hook is an instruction, not a claim:** "Take your [salary / annual income] and multiply it by 0.[X]". The viewer becomes the person doing the maths. There is no greeting, no "most people don't know", and no tease.
- **The first payoff lands before 3 s**, and every later slot pays off again about every 4 to 7 s.
- **Ego and anxiety topics:** "what you ACTUALLY make" (implying you are wrong about it) and buying a car (a large, feared purchase). A short closing line reframes the result: "Most people make 20% less per hour than they actually think."
- **Neither episode loops.** Both end in a hard stop on a follow CTA or a keyword CTA.

## 9. What transfers to "Back of the Envelope" (from this account only)

These are observations from 2 watched TikToks, 1 mirrored IG episode and 12 IG covers. They are not tested rules.

1. **Keep a fixed template grid and change only the accent colour per episode.** Header 11 to 18%, list slots at 32, 43, 53 and 64%, captions at about 71%. Viewers recognise the series in the first frame.
2. **Open on the first instruction with a round constant**, for example "Take your salary and multiply it by 0.7". Put the result on screen by 3 s.
3. **Show the empty numbered slots from frame 1.** The visible "2. 3. 4." is the retention device.
4. **Type every formula, then replace it with the answer in the same slot,** so the finished screen is a saveable cheat sheet.
5. **Reuse one example salary across the series** (he uses $72,000) and keep the constants consistent between steps. His 3M reel mixes 50-week and 52-week years.
6. **Give the series a name of its own** rather than the generic "how to be financially free (in N steps)" that 20 of the 57 other outliers in our search used in Jul to Aug 2026.
7. **One topic per episode, kept concrete:** pay per hour, a car, what you can spend.
8. **Close with a one-line reframe** ("20% less per hour than you think"). A keyword CTA is optional: the 3M reel used a plain "follow".
