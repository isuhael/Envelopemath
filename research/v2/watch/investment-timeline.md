### The parts of the formula, and what each one does (my reading, from these 5 posts)
1. **"POV: You"**: second person, past tense. It drops the viewer into a past decision they could have made, so the line graph becomes "your" timeline. Every post opens this way, and the handle names the idea (investment_timeline).
2. **"invested in [Company]"**: a brand everyone knows (Monster, Crocs, GoPro, NVIDIA). No ticker and no finance jargon in the title.
3. **"instead of paying"**: turns an ordinary purchase into an opportunity cost. The verb is "paying", not "buying", which makes it sound like money leaving.
4. **"[price]"**: one concrete, small, familiar number: $3/day, $50, $400, $599. It is the only number in the title. The result is never in the title.
5. **"for [product]"**: in 4 of 5 posts this is the company's own product, so you were already handing them your money. The odd one out (NVIDIA vs coffee) is the smallest post.
6. **Nostalgia or culture object** (Variant B): Lightning McQueen Crocs (with a #lightningmcqueen tag), the GeForce 256, a GoPro. These are objects people remember buying or wanting.
7. **Outcome hidden**: the title promises a comparison, not a result. In at least one post (GoPro, the biggest) the result is a loss, according to vidIQ's concept line.

## 5. What this means for Back of the Envelope (from this channel only)

1. **Hook template worth testing:** "POV: You [put the money in X] instead of paying $[small familiar price] for [X's own product]."
   - Use one number in the title: the input, never the result.
   - Make the company and the product the same brand, so the irony closes on itself.
2. **Prefer a one-off purchase of an iconic or nostalgic object** (a GeForce 256, Lightning McQueen Crocs, a GoPro) over a generic daily habit. At this account the one-off variant made 3 of 5 posts and 80% of the views (7.65M / 9.53M, check). Treat this as a test hypothesis: it rests on n = 5.
3. **Don't give away the direction.** The account's biggest post is a loss. A rough-but-right channel can do this honestly by running the same template on companies whose products thrived while their stocks sank. The surprise then comes from the maths, not from a clickbait claim.
4. **Look elements we can confirm:**
   - a single static title card made of a sentence, a product photo or logo and a money-bag icon, over a line graph of value vs money spent;
   - 60 s, one continuous animation, no cuts, no face, no voice;
   - an upbeat, recognisable music track;
   - built to loop.

   Everything finer (palette, type, how the final number is shown) is **unknown** for this account, so take those decisions from the benchmark channels whose videos were actually watched.
5. **The caption is just the title plus a fixed hashtag tail.** The account drives no visible comments or CTA, so community tactics have to come from other benchmarks.
