## Our analysis (Envelope Math lens)

**What drove 1.83M views (890x outlier):**
- It answers one of the most-searched money curiosities ("what does a million look like?") with **no words**, so it works in any country.
- The **running counter synced to a physical action** is the whole show. It is the simplest possible math device (repeated addition) but it gives the viewer a reason to stay to the last frame.
- 25 s, one take, no voice: cheap to make and tight enough that people rewatch it.

**Gaps that Envelope Math can own:**
- **No "so what".** The number is shown but never put next to anything. Envelope Math's job is to add one comparison line at the payoff: "€1M = X years of the median salary" or "€1M at 4% = €Y/year" (only with sourced figures).
- **Only one denomination.** The obvious remix, as vidIQ notes, is the denomination ladder: the same €1M in €5 notes vs €50 vs €500, shown as stack height or weight. That is an estimation problem (notes x thickness = height; notes x ~1 g = weight), which is exactly our territory.
- **Prop cash raises authenticity doubts.** A faceless envelope/animation version never claims to be real cash. Animated stacks can be physically accurate *and* honest. Envelope cash-stuffing visuals (envelopes filling up) are an on-brand alternative to prop bricks.

**Steal / adapt for Envelope Math:**
1. **"What X looks like" + running counter:** a top badge counting up as units drop, with an ASMR thud per unit. Use envelopes as the unit: "1 envelope = $1,000. How many envelopes is a median house?"
2. **Denomination / unit ladder:** the same amount in different units (bills, gold bars, paychecks, hours of work), each sized by back-of-envelope math.
3. **Scale object at the end:** finish on the pile next to a familiar object (a fridge, a car, a person) for the "whoa" moment, then cut back to one unit to loop.
4. **Wordless versions for global reach:** numbers-only overlays so the same render works on all three platforms and in all languages.
