- **Pacing:** ~12 cuts in 37 s, about 3.2 cuts per 10 s.
- **Visual style:** hybrid faceless — stock/B-roll + AI-generated realistic stills + 2D cut-out overlays + satisfying/ASMR workshop clips. No talking head.

## Our analysis (Envelope Math lens)

**What actually drove 7.19M views (1,145x outlier):**
- The format is "rags-to-riches founder story in 35 s" — the number "billion" does the heavy lifting as a *status* word, not a calculation. The hook packs a whole story arc into one spoken line: low status (peon), small trigger (noticed a problem), huge outcome (billion-dollar brand).
- It runs on a **series badge** ("Day 2 of Excellent Indian Businesses"). Series framing is a recurring pattern in viral faceless finance shorts: it signals more to come and earns follows.
- Retention comes from **sensory B-roll** (gross glue, then satisfying glue), not from the information. The ASMR roller shot sits right where retention would normally sag (0:25).
- Regional-language voiceover plus a beloved local brand gives it a huge home audience that shares on WhatsApp.

**Where it is weak, and where Envelope Math fits:**
- There is no math at all. "Billion" is never unpacked. The obvious upgrade is an envelope estimate that makes "billion" concrete, for example "How many tubs of glue does it take to be worth a billion?" (price per tub x tubs per carpenter per year x number of carpenters = revenue, then a multiple to get value). That turns a vague status claim into a "wait, really?" number people argue about in the comments.
- AI-generated "realistic" stills of the founder are a credibility risk. A hand-drawn envelope sketch is honest about being an illustration and is on-brand.
- No loop. An envelope format can end on the same number it opened with ("...and that's how a glue tub becomes $1,000,000,000").

**Steal / adapt for Envelope Math:**
1. **Status-inversion opener + a number:** "[Low-status person/thing] -> [huge number]" in the first sentence, then *show the math* on the envelope. Example shape: "A ₹X tub of glue -> a $Y billion company. Here's the napkin math." (Fill in X and Y only from sourced figures.)
2. **Series badge:** "Envelope #N" in the corner of every video, like "Day 2 of ...", to drive follows.
3. **Visceral -> satisfying visual contrast:** pair the "before" (messy, costly) with the "after" (clean) at the payoff moment, synced to the number reveal.
4. **Unit-economics reveal:** the business-story niche lacks numbers; "how many [units] = $1B" is an open lane for us.
