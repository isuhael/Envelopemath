## 1. vidIQ walkthrough (verbatim, lightly reformatted)

### Scene-by-scene breakdown

- **00:00 - 00:05 (the whole video)**
  - **Visual:** A static vertical camera shot of lined paper with handwritten text in blue and black ink. The second line is highlighted with a teal/cyan marker. Nothing moves: no writing action and no animation. It is one still frame.
  - **On-screen text (exact):**
    - `Answer without Googling...` (double underlined)
    - `The answer is not 300 !` (cyan highlight)
    - `500 divided by half`
    - `plus 50 is ?`
  - **Audio/spoken words:** Completely silent. No voiceover and no background track.

### Hook (first 3 seconds)
- **On-screen text:** "Answer without Googling..." / "The answer is not 300 !" / "500 divided by half plus 50 is ?"
- **Spoken words:** None (silent video).

### Numbers and calculations
- **300**, on line 2 with a cyan highlight and an exclamation mark. It rules out the intuitive wrong answer up front (500 / 2 + 50 = 300).
- **500**, written as digits on line 3.
- **"half"**, written as a word rather than 1/2 or 0.5. The word makes the expression ambiguous (500 / 0.5 = 1000).
- **50**, written as digits on line 4.
- **Implicit correct answer:** 500 / (1/2) + 50 = 1000 + 50 = **1050**. The video never shows it.

### Technical and creative characteristics
- **Pacing:** 0 cuts per 10 s. One static shot loops over 5 s.
- **Visual style:** Handwriting on lined notebook paper (pen and highlighter).
- **Text styling:** Neat print/cursive hybrid in blue and black ink. Teal chisel-tip highlighter on line 2. Double underline under the header.
- **Music/SFX:** None (silent).
- **Tension and payoff:** The warning "The answer is not 300!" creates tension immediately by challenging the viewer's mental math. **The video gives no payoff.** It ends on "?", so the viewer has to work out the answer or check the comments.
- **Loop:** Loops seamlessly because the frame never changes.
- **Comment, share and save triggers:** Argument over "divided by half" vs "divided in half" or "divided by two". Viewers post answers (1050, 300, 550, etc.) and argue grammar vs math rules.
- **CTA:** Implied by the header "Answer without Googling...".
- **Total length:** 5.0 s.

### Why it went viral (vidIQ)
- **Instant cognitive dissonance:** The brain computes 500/2 + 50 = 300 automatically, and the video rules out 300 straight away.
- **Linguistic ambiguity and rage bait:** Mathematically, "divided by half" means / 0.5 (= 1000). Colloquially it can mean "cut in half" (/ 2 = 250). The disagreement fills the comment section.
- **Zero watch friction:** At 5 s, retention goes past 100% because viewers pause or rewatch while they solve it.
- **Low-tech authenticity:** The handwritten notebook looks like native user content, not an ad.
- **Open-ended format:** With no answer given, viewers check the comments to see whether 1050 is right.

### What a competitor could do better (vidIQ)
- Show the pen writing the problem in real time, or animate the breakdown, to add movement and watch time.
- Add a TTS voiceover and a suspense track so the video also works with sound on.
- Add a two-part payoff: a 3 s countdown, then the explanation (500 / 1/2 = 1000, then 1000 + 50 = 1050).

## 2. Analyst notes (my own analysis)

**Math check.** 500 / 0.5 = 1000, and 1000 + 50 = 1050. Correct by standard order of operations. vidIQ lists 300 and 550 among the answers viewers post. 300 comes from reading it as "500 halved, plus 50". Where 550 comes from is unclear (maybe 500 + 50 with "divided by half" ignored). vidIQ could not see the comment section, so none of this was measured. The key point: **the video works because two readings both feel correct.** That is a deliberately ambiguous problem, not a hard one.

**Structural anatomy (the formula):**
1. A **permission frame** ("Answer without Googling...") turns watching into a test.
2. A **pre-emptive negation** ("The answer is not 300!") names the wrong answer before the viewer reaches it. Viewers who got 300 feel caught out, and viewers who got 1050 feel smart. Both groups comment.
3. A **2-line problem** using one ambiguous word ("half").
4. **No answer.** The comment section is the payoff.
5. **About 5 s and silent.** Instagram counts rewatches, and a still frame costs almost nothing to make.

**Ratio to note:** 9.3M views on a 14.1K-follower account (about 660 views per follower), with zero production. That makes it the most cost-efficient format in the whole scan.

**Risks if we copy it:**
- It is pure arithmetic trivia. The account (@mathsgenius222) builds no finance brand, and a viewer who watched this learns nothing about the creator. Our version needs a **money** ambiguity to build brand equity.
- Unresolved ambiguity bait can read as low-effort or manipulative when overused. Rotating "trick" riddles with "honest Fermi estimate" riddles keeps trust.
- vidIQ could not see the comment count or comment composition. That the comments are "math police" arguments is inferred from the format and the caption ("Everyone keeps saying 300... But they're all wrong"), not measured.

**How Back of the Envelope can beat it:**
1. **Write it on an envelope, not notebook paper.** That one change makes the format ours, and the envelope becomes the recognisable brand frame.
2. **Use a money ambiguity with real stakes**, not grammar. Candidates where the intuitive answer is wrong:
   - "Lose 50%, then gain 50%. Are you back to even?" (Intuitive: yes. Real: down 25%.)
   - "A 20% discount, then 20% tax. Same price?" (No: 0.8 x 1.2 = 0.96.)
   - "A bat and ball cost $1.10..." style price puzzles.
   - "Salary up 10% for two years. Is that 20%?" (No: 21%.)
   All of these teach something real, which serves our "rough but right" promise.
3. **Keep the 5-7 s silent loop for the puzzle post, and add a reveal post.** Put the answer in a pinned comment or a follow-up "Envelope answer" short, ideally 6-12 s, that shows the envelope being flipped over with the math on the back. That turns one viral post into a two-post sequence and gives a reason to follow.
4. **Keep the pre-emptive negation** ("It's NOT $X!") because it is the strongest element here. Pair it with a caption naming the popular wrong answer.
5. **Make it a series** ("Envelope Puzzle #N") so a viewer who solved one wants the next.
