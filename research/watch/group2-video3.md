## Every number / calculation and how it is visualised

- **Base:** "1 Million" salary (currency never specified; props are US $100 bills). Visualised as one thick physical cash stack in a POV hand.
- **Tax share per country:** a single big percentage in yellow outlined text + a physical split of the stack (the presenter literally removes the government's share and hands back the rest).
  - India 30% -> 70% back
  - Canada 50% -> 50% back
  - UK 40% -> 60% back
  - USA 26% -> 74% back
  - Singapore 24% -> 76% back
  - UAE "NO TAX" -> 100% kept (pocketed)
- No arithmetic is ever written out; the "calculation" is the size of the cash pile that remains.

**Accuracy note (my analysis, not from the video):** each country is reduced to one percentage with no stated basis (marginal vs effective, which year, which province/state, currency). The figures are not consistent with one methodology — e.g. "USA 26%" is not the federal top marginal rate and ignores state tax. This looseness is a feature for engagement (comment wars) but a liability for a brand built on "rough but right". Any figures we use must be checked against current official tax tables before scripting.

- **Pacing:** ~2 cuts per 10 s (6 scenes in ~30 s, ~4–5 s per country) — steady, rhythmic "list" cadence.
- **Visual style:** real-world street skit, first-person POV hand interacting with an on-camera presenter. Not faceless.
- **Text styling:** impact-style bold sans, bright yellow fill, heavy black outline, centred; country flags as lower-third graphics.

## My analysis for Envelope Math

**The core device:** *one fixed pile of money, repeatedly divided, country by country.* The viewer's eye tracks "how much is left in the hand". It is a ranking/countdown format disguised as a skit — the brain wants to see the last item.

**Mechanics to steal:**
- **Same input, different outputs** (one salary -> six take-homes). Easy to extend: same rent across cities, same $1,000 across decades, same salary across careers.
- **Physical split as the visual math** — the pile size IS the answer.
- **Descending order to a 0% or 100% extreme** as the payoff.
- **Flag/emoji lower thirds** for instant context with no narration needed (works muted / across languages — this is a big reason it scales internationally).

**How Envelope Math can do it better / uniquely ours:**
1. **Envelopes instead of a hand-held stack.** Start with 10 envelopes = $1,000,000 (each envelope = $100k). For each country, the taxman pulls out N envelopes. Counting envelopes = instant, honest rounding to the nearest 10% — that is real back-of-the-envelope precision.
2. **Be "rough but right":** pick one clearly stated basis (e.g. effective tax on a $100k single-filer salary, 2026 tables) and write it in small text on the envelope flap. Credibility is our differentiator vs. this video's loose numbers. Still invite "what's yours?" comments.
3. **Add the second envelope:** after tax, a second pass shows what the remaining envelopes buy (rent, healthcare) — answering the #1 comment objection on screen and creating a natural Part 2.
4. **Loop it:** end with the last envelope being sealed and flipped over to reveal the next country's flag = the first frame.
5. **Faceless** — no presenter needed; hands-and-envelopes top-down shot or 2D animation.
