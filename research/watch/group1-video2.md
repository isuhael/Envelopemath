## 2. Analyst notes (my own analysis)

**Math check (arithmetic only).** 237.5M x 0.40 = 95.0M qualified views. 95.0M / 1,000 = 95,000. x $8 = $760,000. 4 x $75,000 = $300,000. $760,000 + $300,000 = $1,060,000. The arithmetic holds. **The inputs are assumptions.** The $8 RPM and the "40% qualified" share are the creator's assumptions, and the $75k/deal and 4 deals/month are guesses. This step did not verify any of them. The $8 RPM is the most contestable: creators have publicly self-reported TikTok Creator Rewards payouts well below that level, so it would need sourcing before we used anything like it. The comment section (per vidIQ) also argues about it. **This is exactly the opening a "rough but right" channel can take: show a low/high range instead of one confident number.**

**Structure as a ratio of runtime (152 s):**
- 0-18 s (12%): hook plus series branding.
- 18-79 s (40%): biography. **No numbers at all for about 60 s.**
- 79-149 s (46%): data, then calculator, then the reveal.
- The payoff at 140 s is **92% of the way through.** The headline number is held to the end.

It still got 908x outlier performance. My reading is that the **series identity** (Episode 77 of a named, logo'd segment), a **famous subject**, and a creator with a recognisable face carry the retention. This was not measured. A faceless channel will not have the face, so our version must front-load the number or range to buy the attention the face buys him.

**The core device is a Fermi estimate, and that is our brand.** Structure: observable input (public views) x assumed conversion (% qualified) x assumed rate (RPM) + a second income stream (deals x price) = monthly total. That is literally back-of-the-envelope math, which is why the outlier scan rated "fermi-estimate" the highest-median-outlier pattern (533x median, per research/raw/ig-tiktok-outliers.md).

**Comment engines present:**
1. "You missed X" (the CTA invites it).
2. "Your RPM is wrong" (an assumption that is contestable on purpose, or by accident).
3. "Do [creator] next" (series request, which feeds future content).

**How Back of the Envelope can beat it:**
1. **Number first, story second.** Open on the range with a question ("Jordan makes somewhere between $X and $Y a month. Here's the envelope."), or open on the envelope with the total crossed out. Cut the 60 s biography to one 5 s line.
2. **Show 3-4 inputs as envelope lines, each with a low and a high value**, then multiply both columns. A **range** is more honest and invites less "you're wrong" than a single number, while still inviting "I think it's closer to the low end" comments.
3. **Cite inputs on screen** (small source tags under each line) to build trust a face can't.
4. **Run at 35-60 s, not 152 s.** Faceless retention cannot lean on charisma. If a long cut is wanted, it should keep a running total visible at all times.
5. **Keep the recurring-series mechanic** ("Envelope Estimate #N: who's next?"). The named series is part of what makes the format work.
6. **Widen the subject pool beyond creators** to pro athletes, a local taco truck, the ice-cream van, the Taylor Swift tour, a Costco hot dog stand. Any business can get the "envelope estimate" treatment.
