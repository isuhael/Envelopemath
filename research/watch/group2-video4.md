## My analysis for Envelope Math

**The core device is "commit-then-reveal" estimation:** the viewer is forced to make a guess (Fermi estimate) before the truth lands. That is exactly what back-of-the-envelope math is. Seeing four other people guess wrong (anchoring high, then lower) makes the viewer feel smart or surprised either way.

**Mechanics to steal:**
- **Easy question first, hard question second** (What phone is it? -> What's it worth now?).
- **Multiple guesses at different distances from the truth** — the viewer ranks themselves against them.
- **A neutral "truth source"** for the reveal (here an app; for us, a data source / receipt).
- **Big before/after gap** (bought for X, worth Y) — depreciation and inflation are natural fits.

**How Envelope Math can do it better / uniquely ours:**
1. **The sealed envelope IS the reveal.** Like an awards-show envelope: "The answer is in the envelope. Guess first." Pause, then open. This is an ownable, faceless, repeatable visual that no game show has — and it's literally our brand.
2. **Show the estimation, not just the guesses.** Instead of four celebrities, one hand scribbles a 3-step Fermi estimate on the envelope ("phones lose ~1/3 a year -> 3 years -> keep ~30%") in ~10 s, then the envelope is opened to reveal the real number. Viewers learn a reusable rule of thumb AND get the guessing game.
3. **Score the estimate:** a red "within 2x = envelope-approved" stamp. Envelope math's honest standard ("right order of magnitude") becomes a recurring mini-ritual viewers can play in comments.
4. **Explicit comment prompt + loop:** "Write your guess before I open it" on screen at 0:02, and end on the next sealed envelope (next episode's question) to loop / create series pull.
5. **Keep it 20–30 s** — we don't need the 48 s studio format without celebrity banter.
