## 2. Analyst notes (my own analysis)

**Math check.** $1,500.00 + $104.83 = $1,604.83. $5,416.33 - $1,604.83 = **$3,811.50**, which the video rounds to "$3,800". That is a correct back-of-the-envelope rounding.

**Envelope math the video did NOT do (our derivations, not claims made in the video):**
- Annualised take-home: $5,416.33 x 26 biweekly checks = **$140,824.58/year net**, assuming every check is the same size, which is unknown. The video never says this number. It is the single most shocking figure in the clip, and it is left unsaid.
- If both checks a month are saved or invested as he describes ("invest one full paycheck each month and save the other"), and the card payments are typical, he is setting aside roughly $3,800 x 26, or about **$99K a year**. **That rests on assumptions we have not verified**, so it shows what an envelope-style follow-up could say, not a fact about him.
- The gross pay, hours and overtime are unknown. vidIQ notes that this is the top comment question.

**Why the hook works (structure):**
1. **An oddly specific number in parentheses, "($5,416.33)".** The cents read as a real deposit, not a rounded brag. The outlier scan found the same device across several top performers ($713.59, $2,421.88, $17.50 after taxes, $27.40 a day).
2. **An identity and age tag, "as a 20 year old nurse".** It triggers comparison ("I'm 20 too", "I'm a nurse and I don't make that"). Comparison drives comments.
3. **A familiar series format, "budget my paycheck".** It is a recognised TikTok genre, so viewers know the payoff structure from the title alone.
4. **Proof assets** (bank notification, payment screenshots, badge) **pre-empt "fake" comments**, which still appear but in smaller numbers.

**Weaknesses (our opening):**
- The math is spread over about 70 s of talk. Viewers have to hold the numbers in their heads.
- No visual running balance, no allocation chart, no envelope split.
- The most interesting derived numbers (annual take-home, hourly equivalent, savings rate) are never shown.
- 107 s with 0-1 cuts per 10 s works only because a real person is telling a real story. A faceless account cannot rely on that.

**How Back of the Envelope can beat it (faceless):**
1. **"Envelope a real paycheck."** Open on an oddly specific number written on an envelope ("$2,416.73 biweekly, age 24, dental hygienist" or a viewer-submitted check). Then split the cash into labelled envelopes on screen (Rent / Car / Food / Invest / Fun), with **a running balance in the corner**.
2. **Do the annualising out loud:** "x 26 = $62,835 a year take-home. That's $30.21 an hour after tax." Each unit conversion is a mini-reveal. The outlier scan rated unit-conversion ladders the highest-outlier hook type.
3. **Finish on the envelope that matters:** "If the Invest envelope gets $400 every check for 10 years at about 7%, it's roughly $X." That gives a save-worthy payoff the original lacks. Compute it exactly at script time.
4. **Make it a series on viewer submissions:** "Comment your take-home and job, and I'll envelope it." The outlier scan found a personalised-reply CTA (@itsjoeyviola) at 125.1x on 3.7K followers.
5. **Keep it to 30-50 s** with a cut or new number every 2-3 s.
6. **Keep the proof aesthetic** (a realistic-looking deposit notification as a stylised graphic). But **never fabricate a real person's bank record.** Use clearly hypothetical or clearly sourced, viewer-consented numbers.
