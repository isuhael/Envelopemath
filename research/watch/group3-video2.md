## Our analysis (Envelope Math lens)

**What drove 5.19M views (1,131x outlier):**
- It is a **6-second, one-formula short**. The full "math" fits in one sentence: buy for X, earn Y per week, Z per year, times 10. Short enough to loop, simple enough to do in your head, and wrong enough to argue with.
- **The math is deliberately incomplete** and that is the engine. It shows gross revenue as "made", skips every cost, doesn't subtract the purchase price, and uses 48 weeks for a "year". Every omission is a reason to comment. Comment velocity is what pushes a 6 s clip into millions.
- **Proof prop:** cash on the hood of the actual car is a trust shortcut that replaces any on-screen working.

**Where Envelope Math wins:**
- We can run the **"envelope audit"** of exactly this kind of claim: put the hype formula at the top of the envelope, then cross out and subtract the missing costs line by line (insurance, maintenance, depreciation, downtime/vacancy, platform fee, taxes), ending on a realistic net and a realistic payback period. Same hook number, honest ending. That gives us the side-hustle crowd's attention without peddling a fantasy, and the "the real number is..." twist drives comments from both camps.
- **Keep it short.** The lesson from this one is that the whole idea should be readable in one breath. An audit version can be 15–25 s if each subtraction is a beat (strike-through + "-$X" + SFX) and the final number lands just before the loop.
- **Never present illustrative costs as facts.** Any insurance/maintenance numbers we use must be sourced (or clearly labelled "assume") on screen; "assume" lines are themselves very Fermi and on-brand.

**Steal / adapt for Envelope Math:**
1. **One-sentence formula hook in frame 1** ("Buy X for $A, rent it for $B/week = $C/year"), fully readable before the first loop.
2. **"Now imagine 10 of them" scaler** as a second beat: multiplication by a round number is the cheapest dopamine in finance shorts.
3. **The Envelope Audit twist:** we add the step they skipped (costs, taxes, time value, risk) and land on the "real" number. Use a red pen strike-through on the envelope.
4. **Payback period as the hero stat:** "$3,000 / $400 = 7.5 weeks" is a cleaner, more surprising number than annual revenue, and it is pure envelope math.
5. **Deliberate debate surface:** leave one clearly-labelled assumption (e.g. "assume 40 rental weeks") for viewers to argue about. That gets the comments without being misleading.
