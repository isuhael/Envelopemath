## 5. The Envelope Split

**Series:** *Envelope My Paycheck* · **Format brand:** Envelope Math · **Lane:** Envelope (31–39 s masters, cut per platform)
**Written:** 2026-10-07. The writer checked every real-world input with WebSearch on that date; sources and dates sit with each teaser. Every number was recomputed by the script in [Math check](#math-check). **QA pass (same day):** see [Verification log](#verification-log). Two inputs (the iPhone 18 Pro price and NAR's August 2026 median) could not be re-opened by QA and must be re-checked before publishing.
**Specs:** `engine/specs/05-envelope-split-a.json`, `-b.json`, `-c.json` · **Contact sheets:** `engine/out/sheets/05-envelope-split-{a,b,c}.png`

One real paycheck goes into four labelled cash envelopes on screen. Then we add the part nobody adds: **how long one envelope takes to fill.**

---

### Why it goes viral

**The mechanism.**

1. **It's participatory.** Every viewer quietly runs the split on their own pay while watching, so attention is self-generated.
2. **The number is oddly specific.** "$5,416.33" reads as a real deposit, not a brag.
3. **The system shows before it's explained.** @moneyletter's four labelled cups are on the desk before he says a word.
4. **It runs as a numbered open loop.** "Number one… number five" tells the viewer how much is left.
5. **The emotional arc goes from restriction to permission.** It opens on "do not spend a single dollar" and lands on "$600 guilt-free".
6. **The algorithm is fed by saves, comments and the cash.** People save frameworks. They comment their own number ("comment your take-home") or say "that's impossible in my city". And cash dropping into containers is ASMR.

**What the format lacks.** Across the whole paycheck-split cluster:

- nobody **times** an envelope;
- nobody shows **where the take-home number comes from**;
- the YouTube versions without math stall.

### How the originals do it

**1. @se33y, "budget my paycheck ($5,416.33) as a 20 year old nurse"** (TikTok, 107 s (1:47) as watched; the outlier sheet lists 106 s; 3.2M, 591.4x; watched: `research/watch/group1-video3.md`)
- **Nails:**
  - The hook is a three-part machine: an oddly specific number in parentheses, an identity and age tag ("20 year old nurse"), and a known genre ("budget my paycheck").
  - Proof assets (a deposit notification, payment screenshots, a badge) head off "fake" comments.
  - Pay-transparency communities share it.
- **Misses:**
  - The math is spread over about 70 s of car-vlog talk with no running balance and no allocation visual.
  - The most shocking derived numbers are never said: $5,416.33 × 26 = $140,824.58 a year take-home, and the savings rate.
  - At 107 s with 0–1 cuts per 10 s, it works only because a real person is telling a real story. A faceless account can't borrow that.

**2. @moneyletter, "How to split your paycheck (without going broke)"** (Instagram, 72 s, ~3.0M, 316x; watched: `research/watch/group6-video1.md`)
- **Nails:**
  - Frame 1 shows four labelled cups (50/15/5/30) **before a word is spoken**.
  - The negative-command hook ("do not spend a single dollar") lands on a universal moment.
  - Each cash drop gets a dollar overlay.
  - The permission payoff ($600 guilt-free) is the emotional peak.
  - A "Comment BUDGET" DM funnel drives the comments.
- **Misses:**
  - It takes 20 s to reach the first calculation.
  - There are no SFX on the drops.
  - **The pay frequency is never stated.**
  - The split is frozen: nothing says how long any cup takes to fill, or what it becomes.
  - It has no loop.

**3. Joey Viola, "How I Split Every Paycheck (The Exact 5 Percentages)"** (YouTube, 40 s, 815,565, 565.9x on 1.48K subs; watched: `research/watch/group4-video2.md`)
- **Nails:**
  - Zero preamble: the first words are step 1.
  - Each line morphs from formula to result (`$3,500 * .55` → `$1925/m`), a tiny calculator moment five times over.
  - The numbered 1-of-5 loop holds viewers, and the spoken "Save for later" fits a save-worthy format.
- **Misses:**
  - **Categories are never labelled on screen**, so the meaning lives in the audio.
  - The title says "paycheck" but the math uses "monthly income".
  - It has no SFX, no loop, and no payoff beyond the list.

### The Envelope Math upgrade

**What we replicate:**
- the oddly specific number and the identity tag in frame 1 (se33y);
- labelled containers, a cash drop per beat and the restriction-to-permission arc (moneyletter);
- the formula-to-result morph with no preamble and a save-worthy rule (Joey Viola);
- the personalised-reply CTA ("comment your take-home", 125.1x).

**What we improve:**
- **Labels persist on screen.** Each envelope carries its name and percentage ("OOPS 5%"), so the frame is screenshot-able for saves.
- **We show where the paycheck comes from.** Every episode states the pay frequency (every 2 weeks) and derives take-home on screen: a counter ticks gross down to net after 2026 federal tax and FICA. The assumptions sit on an **ASSUME: sticky**, and every input is sourced and dated.
- **The split happens in your head.** We teach the four shortcuts once and reuse them: 10% = move the decimal; 5% = half that; 15% = both; 30% = three tens; 50% = half.
- **It's faster.** A dollar figure is on screen in frame 1 (the hook is pre-rolled, so the very first frame is complete), and the first envelope fills by 4.4–5.9 s; moneyletter took 20 s to reach a calculation. Each cash drop gets its own foley.
- **It's honest about rounding.** Envelopes show whole dollars, the pinned comment shows the cents, and red pen marks the rounding.
- **It ends on a loop or a re-hook**, not a sign-off.

**The uniquely-ours twist: the Fill Line.** Every split ends by **timing one envelope**:
- How many paychecks until it reaches a real target? The working fits in 3 ballpoint lines.
- A row of paychecks fills until the envelope is full.
- A verdict stamp lands.

**Nobody in the corpus does time-to-fill** (research report §6, whitespace 3: "nobody computes envelope ratios, time-to-fill…").

**What rotates, so nothing becomes template bulk:**
- **The envelope that gets timed:** OOPS → FUN → FUTURE.
- **The paycheck:** median worker → minimum-wage first job → nurse.

Each episode is new math, not a re-skin, which matters under YouTube's Oct 2026 originality update.

**The house split** is MUST 50 / FUTURE 15 / OOPS 5 / FUN 30, built on Fidelity's 50/15/5 guideline:
- Fidelity puts 50% of take-home on essentials and 5% of take-home into short-term savings.
- Fidelity's 15% is of *pre-tax* pay including any employer match. We simplify it to 15% of take-home for the envelope, and the pinned comment says so.
- The remaining 30% is FUN.

We present it as "here's the math on a common rule", never "you should".

**Series name:** **Envelope My Paycheck.** It riffs on TikTok's proven "budget my paycheck" genre and comes with its own CTA: *"Comment your take-home and job. I'll envelope it."*

**Title template:** `[Who]'s $[take-home] paycheck vs. [goal]: [time-to-fill question] (Envelope My Paycheck No. 05X)`. Or the claim form: `[Paycheck]. One envelope takes [N months] to fill.` Keep the number in the title, which matches the on-screen hook.

**Shared assumptions behind every take-home figure:**
- single filer, standard deduction ($16,100 for 2026);
- 2026 federal brackets: 10% to $12,400, 12% to $50,400, 22% to $105,700;
- FICA 7.65% (6.2% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base, plus 1.45% Medicare);
- Texas, so no state income tax;
- no 401(k), insurance or other deductions;
- paid every two weeks (26 paychecks);
- take-home = (gross − the year's federal income tax − FICA) ÷ 26. Actual withholding can differ by a few dollars.

Recomputes every number that appears on screen, in the voice-over or in a pinned comment,

  BLS median annual wage, registered nurses, May 2025 ......................... $97,550

  NAR 2025 Profile: first-time buyers' median down payment 10%, median age 40
  Fidelity 50/15/5: 50% of take-home on essentials, 5% of take-home to short-term savings;
  Fidelity emergency fund: 3 to 6 months of essential expenses
"""
from decimal import Decimal as D, ROUND_HALF_UP
import math
from fractions import Fraction as F

def c(x):  # round to cents
    return D(x).quantize(D('0.01'), ROUND_HALF_UP)

def d(x):  # round to whole dollars (what the stuff envelopes show)
    return int(D(x).quantize(D('1'), ROUND_HALF_UP))

def pct_off(est, exact):
    return abs(D(est) - D(exact)) / D(exact) * 100  # print with 2 decimals: 0.515% is NOT 'within 0.5%'

STD = D(16100)
BRACKETS = [(D(12400), D('0.10')), (D(50400), D('0.12')), (D(105700), D('0.22')), (D(201775), D('0.24'))]
SS_RATE, SS_BASE, MED_RATE = D('0.062'), D(184500), D('0.0145')
PAYS = 26  # paid every two weeks

def federal_tax(gross):
    taxable, tax, lo = max(D(0), gross - STD), D(0), D(0)
    for hi, rate in BRACKETS:
        if taxable > lo:
            tax += (min(taxable, hi) - lo) * rate
        lo = hi
    return tax

def take_home(gross):
    tax = federal_tax(gross)
    fica = min(gross, SS_BASE) * SS_RATE + gross * MED_RATE
    return tax, fica, (gross - tax - fica) / PAYS
